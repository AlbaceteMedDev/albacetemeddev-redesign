NONCONTACT & NONTHERMAL  ·  ADJUNCTIVE THERAPY

UltraMIST® therapy.

Albacete MedDev is an independent distributor of UltraMIST®, manufactured by SANUWAVE — low-frequency, noncontact, nonthermal ultrasound used as an adjunctive treatment for acute and chronic wounds. Energy reaches the wound through saline, with nothing touching the wound surface. Billed under CPT 97610, with our reimbursement and documentation infrastructure behind it.

How it works

Acoustic energy, delivered without touching the wound.

Low-frequency, noncontact, nonthermal ultrasound. SANUWAVE’s own description of the mechanism is narrow, and we reproduce it as written rather than expanding on it: acoustic waves mechanically stimulate the cells in and below the wound surface.

Mechanism

What the energy does

Acoustic waves mechanically stimulate the cells in and below the wound surface. SANUWAVE cites one reference for that statement — Serena, Ostomy Wound Management 2009;55(1):22–30 — and it supports the mechanism, not a healing rate.

Delivery

Noncontact, saline only

The applicator does not touch the wound surface. The system runs on saline only — no other fluids — through a single-use disposable applicator, and is operated by trained staff.

Scope

What UltraMIST is not

SANUWAVE’s own list: not sharp debridement, not pulse lavage or VersaJet, not an ultrasound contact debrider. A side-by-side product comparison we hold also records no contact debridement, no removal of nonviable tissue, and no osteomyelitis indication. Debridement, where it is needed, stays a separate step.

Indications for use, as SANUWAVE states them: in any type of acute or chronic wound; over exposed tendon, bone, arteries and veins; over metal hardware or staples, and skin grafts. The reimbursement guide describes the system as a wound care therapy for wounds, burns and ulcers — including venous leg and diabetic foot ulcers — that may be eligible for coverage when considered reasonable and medically necessary. Contraindications: in a wound or over an area where cancer cells are present; over electrical implants (i.e., pacemakers); over and around a pregnant uterus.

What we can and cannot show you. The materials we hold from SANUWAVE — the product deck and the 2025 reimbursement and coding guide (MK2039 Rev B) — describe the mechanism, the indications, and the billing pathway. They contain a single clinical reference, Serena, Ostomy Wound Management 2009;55(1):22–30, cited in support of the mechanism statement above. They do not contain controlled outcome data for this device, so nothing on this page should be read as a healing-rate or comparative-outcome claim. Noncontact low-frequency ultrasound is a modality with its own published literature; evidence about the modality is not evidence about this device, and we will not present it as such. Ask us and we will forward whatever study documentation SANUWAVE supplies, so you can assess it yourself.

Patient selection

SANUWAVE’s reimbursement guide states UltraMIST may be utilized for patients for whom:

  • Traditional debridement methods have been unsuccessful, and the patient is contraindicated for sharp or excisional debridement techniques
  • There is a lack of progress observed after a continuous period of 30 days under standard wound care

The product deck states the same criteria in slightly different terms — no significant improvement after 30 days of SOC; too painful for sharp debridement; contraindicated for sharp debridement (e.g., unstageable DTI, blood thinners, hemophilia, non-compliant patient). Review the Local Coverage Determination for 97610 in your state for specific guidance.

Per-session documentation

Each session should capture all five elements. A qualifying wound diagnosis is required.

  • Pain level
  • Measurement of the wound — and any undermining or tunneling
  • Tissue appearance — granulation vs. necrotic tissue
  • Inflammation / redness
  • Drainage

Reassessment rule: Improvement in at least one of these elements needs to be evident after the sixth treatment.

No more than 18 treatments within a six-week period will be considered reasonable and necessary. Therapy may continue beyond 18 treatments if the wound is still responding but has not resolved enough to return to routine standard of care — seek approval from the payer and follow proper reassessment and documentation guidelines. Use is discontinued when the wound has resolved, or at the provider’s discretion.

CMS designated 97610 a “sometimes therapy” code, which can be performed by qualified healthcare professionals outside a certified therapy plan of care. Billed by a hospital, these codes may be paid as non-therapy services for hospital outpatients and typically do not require therapy modifiers; furnished by a qualified therapist under a therapy plan of care (POC), therapy modifiers may be required for payment under the Medicare Physician Fee Schedule. Consult your MAC or private carrier for specific requirements. Coding information from SANUWAVE’s guide is informational only and is not a guarantee of coverage by any payer.

Where it fits

Low-frequency ultrasound, delivered without contact.

Acoustic energy reaching cells in and below the wound surface — used alongside standard wound care, not in place of it.

Noncontact

Nothing touches the wound

The applicator does not contact the wound surface; energy is carried by saline. A product comparison sheet we hold records the therapy as pain-free noncontact. SANUWAVE indicates its use over exposed tendon, bone, arteries, veins, metal hardware, staples and skin grafts.

Adjunctive

An adjunct, not a replacement

SANUWAVE positions UltraMIST as an adjunctive treatment for acute and chronic wounds. It can be used for wound bed preparation prior to graft placement — depending on the appearance of the wound — and resumed after the required waiting period.

Setting

Clinic, office, or mobile

System accessories include a roll stand and adapter plate for clinic and office use, and a custom travel case for mobile practices. The system uses single-use disposable applicators and is operated by trained staff.
