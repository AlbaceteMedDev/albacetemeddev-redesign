The Tissue

Placental tissue is a neomatrix — not a salvaged one.

Placental tissue, previously considered biologic waste following delivery, is a pristine newly formed extracellular matrix (ECM) built to protect and support fetal development. That is the manufacturer’s framing, and it is the honest one: this is an untainted extracellular matrix — essentially a neomatrix.

Origin

Newly formed ECM

The matrix is built during gestation for a single purpose — to protect and support fetal development. The manufacturer’s word for the result is a neomatrix: newly formed, untainted extracellular matrix.

Which layer

Amnion — the fetus-facing membrane

The amnion membrane is a layer of placental tissue, identified as the internal or fetus-facing membrane. Sentry™ SL is that layer, and only that layer.

Physical character

Thin, yet highly compact

The physical and biochemical character of the amnion is a thin, yet highly compact layer of extracellular matrix. That is the manufacturer’s description of the layer, and the same literature positions this graft at shallow-to-moderate wound beds.

Handling property

Elastic and durable

The elasticity and durability of the amnion layer allows it to conform closely to wound surfaces while providing a protective barrier. Conformance and coverage from the same single layer.

Composition

What is actually in the layer.

Amnion membrane is constituted of collagen fibers — predominantly Types I, III, IV and V — along with other ECM proteins, including laminin, fibronectin, proteoglycans and hyaluronic acid.

Why the composition matters at the bedside

Sentry™ allograft tissues retain the structural and functional characteristics of the starting placental membranes. That is the claim the manufacturer makes and the one worth holding them to — the processing is designed to preserve the membrane, not to reconstitute it into something else.

  • Barrier functionThe membrane provides a protective barrier over the wound surface — the primary intended use, stated plainly in the manufacturer’s literature.
  • Close conformanceElasticity and durability allow the layer to conform closely to wound surfaces — the manufacturer’s words, and the extent of the stated handling claim.
  • Thin, yet highly compactThe manufacturer’s description of the amnion layer, verbatim. The literature assigns it no filling or bridging function, and neither do we.
  • Native ECM proteinsCollagen I, III, IV and V with laminin, fibronectin, proteoglycans and hyaluronic acid — the constituents of the starting membrane.
  • Dehydrated formatThe final products are minimally manipulated and dehydrated, packaged in different sizes, and verified terminally sterilized.

A note on what we are not claiming. The manufacturer’s literature describes composition, processing, and intended use. It does not publish a growth-factor assay, a cytokine panel, or comparative outcome data for Sentry™. We will not manufacture those numbers for you — and you should be skeptical of any distributor who does.

Choosing the graft

Sentry™ SL or Palisade™? Depth decides.

Two placental allografts, built differently — separate manufacturers, separate products, separate HCPCS codes, one shared size ladder. They are not a product family; we distribute both. Each manufacturer’s literature positions its graft by wound-bed depth, and that is the cleanest way to make the call in clinic.

How the decision actually gets made

Both products are described by their manufacturers as a barrier or wound cover — Sentry™ for acute and chronic wounds, Palisade™ across a spectrum of clinical needs — and both for a variety of acute or chronic wound applications. The differentiator is the build, and each manufacturer positions its own build by bed depth.

  • Shallow-to-moderate → Sentry™ SLA thin, highly compact single amnion layer, described as conforming closely to wound surfaces while providing a protective barrier. That is the manufacturer’s positioning; the literature states no mechanism beyond close conformance and barrier.
  • Moderate-to-deep → Palisade™The dual-membrane build adds the chorion — a slightly thicker, less compact matrix — and its manufacturer positions it for moderate-to-deep beds.
  • Comparable processingEach manufacturer’s own literature describes a minimally manipulated, dehydrated placental allograft from fully consented full-term c-section donors, terminally sterilized.
  • Different HCPCS codesQ4348 for Sentry™, Q4350 for Palisade™. Never carry one code across to the other product — that is an avoidable audit finding.
  • Identical size ladder2×2, 2×4, 4×4 and 4×8 cm on both, so your sizing habits transfer even when the product does not.
  • Selection is clinicalProduct choice, wound assessment, and application remain the treating clinician’s judgment. We supply the specs, not the plan of care.

Intended Use

A natural biologic wound covering or skin substitute.

Sentry™ Placental-Derived Allograft (361 HCT/Ps) are intended as a natural biologic wound covering or skin substitute for cutaneous wounds. Use by qualified health care professionals is for application in a physician office, outpatient, or inpatient setting.

01 · Indication

Cutaneous wounds

The stated intended use is a wound covering or skin substitute for cutaneous wounds, across a variety of acute or chronic wound applications.

02 · Wound bed

Shallow-to-moderate

The manufacturer positions Sentry™ particularly when applied to shallow-to-moderate wound beds. Deeper beds are where Palisade™ — a dual-membrane allograft from a different manufacturer — is positioned by its own literature.

03 · Who applies

Qualified professionals

Application is by qualified health care professionals, per the Instructions for Use. Product selection and technique stay with the treating clinician.

04 · Where

Office, HOPD, inpatient

Intended for application in a physician office, outpatient, or inpatient setting — the same three places most wound programs already operate.

What the IFU does — and does not — say

The Instructions for Use language is deliberately broad: cutaneous wounds. Sequence LifeScience does not enumerate specific wound etiologies, application intervals, or a maximum number of applications in the product literature we distribute. Anyone who tells you otherwise is filling in blanks.

Treat coverage as a separate question from intended use. Payer policy — not the IFU — determines which wound types, care settings, and application counts are reimbursable in your jurisdiction. Read the full package insert and Instructions for Use before first use; additional details of screening procedures are found there.

Sizing discipline

  • Measure firstRecord length × width and the resulting wound area at every application. The measurement, not the box, is the defensible number.
  • Size to the bedSelect the SKU that covers the wound with the least excess. Over-sizing is a common source of unit-count exposure on per-square-centimeter codes.
  • Document discardIf any portion is not applied, record what was applied and what was discarded, with the reason. Follow your payer’s wastage rules exactly.
  • Log the identifiersSKU, size, lot or serial, and date of application belong in the note — every time, without exception.

How to read that

These references support statements about what amniotic membrane is — its composition, its structure, its behavior as a biomaterial. They do not, on their own, establish a comparative outcome for Sentry™ against any other product.

  • Supported by the referencesComposition and structural claims: collagen types, ECM proteins, thin-but-compact character, elasticity and durability, conformance and barrier function.
  • Manufacturer-statedIntended-use and processing claims — minimally manipulated, dehydrated, terminally sterilized, 361 HCT/P, applied by qualified professionals in office, outpatient or inpatient settings — come from the manufacturer’s literature, not from the two cited references.
  • Not assertedHealing rates, time-to-closure, comparative superiority, or head-to-head performance against any named product. We have no such data for Sentry™ and will not imply we do.
  • Your due diligenceRequest the package insert and Instructions for Use before first use, and read them alongside your MAC’s current coverage position.

Common questions

What to know about Sentry™ SL.

What exactly is Sentry™ SL? +
Sentry™ Placental-Derived Allograft is comprised of a single layer of amniotic membrane, produced using a proprietary tissue processing technique. It is designed to serve as a barrier or wound cover for acute and chronic wounds, and the manufacturer positions it particularly for shallow-to-moderate wound beds. How do I choose between Sentry™ SL and Palisade™? +
Depth. Sentry™ SL is a single layer of amniotic membrane, positioned for shallow-to-moderate wound beds — the amnion is a thin yet highly compact matrix whose elasticity and durability let it conform closely to the wound surface while providing a protective barrier. Palisade™ is a dual-membrane allograft — amnion plus chorion — positioned by its manufacturer for moderate-to-deep wound beds, the chorion being described as a slightly thicker but less compact matrix. Both come in 2×2, 2×4, 4×4 and 4×8 cm. They bill under different HCPCS codes: Q4348 for Sentry™, Q4350 for Palisade™. They are also made by different companies — Sentry™ by Sequence LifeScience, Inc. and Palisade™ by Nvision Biomedical Technologies, Inc. — so treat them as two separate products we distribute, not one product line. See the Palisade™ page for the dual-membrane detail.
Is Sentry™ FDA-approved? +
No — and no one should tell you it is. Sentry™ is a 361 HCT/P: a human cell, tissue, or cellular and tissue-based product regulated as human tissue under 21 CFR 1271. It is not an FDA-approved drug or biologic, and it is not a 510(k)-cleared device. What the manufacturer does state is that the tissue is minimally manipulated, that donors are screened to FDA and American Association of Tissue Banks standards, that recovery is done in partnership with FDA-regulated and accredited recovery organizations, and that products undergo a validated terminal sterilization process.
Which wounds is it indicated for, and who can apply it? +
The Instructions for Use state that Sentry™ Placental-Derived Allograft (361 HCT/Ps) are intended as a natural biologic wound covering or skin substitute for cutaneous wounds.
