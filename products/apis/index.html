SweetBio, Inc. · FDA-cleared · Rx only

APIS® — collagen and Manuka honey in one absorbable sheet.

APIS® from SweetBio, Inc. (Memphis, TN) combines a highly purified collagen derivative (gelatin), Manuka honey, and hydroxyapatite in a single solid sheet that covers and protects the wound, absorbs exudate, and maintains a moist wound environment. It is biodegradable and completely absorbable, so removal at dressing changes is not necessary. Albacete MedDev supplies APIS® to US physicians and wound-care programs with coding and documentation support.

Composition

Three materials, one matrix.

Manuka honey has published antibacterial, MMP-modulating, and growth-factor-upregulating properties — but as a gel or paste it is messy and short-lived on the wound. Collagen buffers destructive proteases, but usually needs added compounds to do more. APIS® synthesizes both, reinforced with hydroxyapatite, into one solid sheet — what SweetBio calls "a simple solution to complex wounds." The mechanism summary below reflects the literature and in vitro data cited in SweetBio's publications; clinical outcomes are graded separately in the evidence section.

Collagen derivative

Purified gelatin

A natural protein matrix with high biocompatibility, good biodegradability, and high water absorption; the literature associates it with accelerated granulation and epithelialization. In the chronic-wound environment it can act as a sacrificial substrate, buffering elevated matrix metalloproteinases such as MMP-2 and MMP-9. Porcine-derived.

Active honey

Manuka honey

High osmolarity, high viscosity, and low pH. Published work shows it inhibits microbial growth, increases fibroblast activity, and has anti-inflammatory effects, while absorbing and holding moisture at the wound. The sheet format delivers it without the mess and short wear time of topical gels and pastes.

Mineral reinforcement

Hydroxyapatite

A naturally occurring calcium-phosphate mineral that gives the sheet its structural reinforcement. It has been reported to support wound healing through re-epithelialization, matrix formation, angiogenesis, and recruitment of macrophages and fibroblasts.

Indications

Where APIS® is indicated — and where it is not.

Per the manufacturer's Instructions for Use (IFU-011), APIS® is indicated for wound management under the supervision of a healthcare professional. Several contraindications follow directly from its composition — screen for collagen, porcine, and honey sensitivities before use.

Indicated wound types

  • Full and partial thickness wounds
  • Pressure ulcers (stages I–IV)
  • Venous stasis ulcers
  • Diabetic ulcers
  • Abrasion and surface wounds
  • Traumatic wounds healing by secondary intention
  • Donor site wounds
  • Surgical wounds

Contraindications

  • Sensitivity to collagen and its derivatives
  • Sensitivity to porcine-derived products
  • Sensitivity to honey
  • Third-degree burns
  • Infection or contamination in the wound site
  • Control of bleeding
  • Children and infant patients under 10 kg / 22 lbs

Per the IFU, do not apply APIS® until bleeding, infection, excessive exudate, and acute swelling are controlled. Do not re-sterilize; discard opened, unused material. Remove APIS® and discontinue use if signs of sensitivity appear.

In practice

Applied in minutes, absorbed in place.

APIS® can be applied from the onset and for the duration of the wound, alongside standard wound-care protocols. Because the sheet is biodegradable and completely absorbable, there is nothing to peel away at follow-up — reassess and reapply as required.

Five steps per the IFU

  • Prepare the wound bed per standard protocols; debride if necessary.
  • Measure the wound and select the size and number of sheets. APIS® may expand slightly in length and width on hydration.
  • Hydrate if needed and place APIS® directly onto the wound.
  • Cover and secure with a suitable secondary dressing to keep the sheet in contact with the wound.
  • Change secondary dressings as needed; inspect the wound per standard protocols and reapply APIS® as required.

SweetBio also positions APIS® for prescription home use — "now available at home," per its clinician flyer — so a course started in clinic can continue between visits under professional supervision.

Clinical evidence

The data, graded honestly.

Three clinical evaluations stand behind APIS®: a randomized controlled pilot trial, a retrospective comparison against standard of care, and a preliminary case series. Designs, sample sizes, and actual endpoints below. SweetBio-affiliated authors contributed to the case series and the retrospective evaluation (disclosed in the publications); the RCT was conducted by Vanderbilt dermatology. Full citations available on request.

Randomized controlled trial · n = 37

Mohs surgical defects — Vanderbilt, 2023

Pilot RCT (Dermatol Surg 2023; NCT04545476). 37 patients healing by secondary intention after Mohs micrographic surgery on the head or distal lower extremities were randomized to standard secondary-intention healing or APIS®-enhanced healing. Result: no significant difference in time to complete re-epithelialization — APIS® was noninferior — with significantly lower pain scores and reduced skin thickness in the APIS® group. This trial shows noninferior closure with less pain, not faster healing.

Retrospective comparative · 47 wounds

Chronic wounds vs standard of care — Wounds, 2023

IRB-approved retrospective evaluation at a level I urban trauma-center wound clinic (Regional One Health, Memphis): 47 chronic wounds in 43 patients, 80% diabetic foot ulcers, wounds ≤9 cm². Most wounds in both groups did not close (26% with APIS®, n=27, vs 40% with standard of care, n=20; nonadherence was the leading reason). Among wounds that did close, mean time to closure was 7.4 weeks with APIS® vs 14.8 weeks with standard of care (P < .05) — the source of SweetBio's "2x faster wound closure" claim.

Case series · n = 12

Lower-extremity ulcers — Int J Low Extrem Wounds, 2020

Preliminary retrospective evaluation across 4 sites, no control group. 12 patients with chronic lower-extremity ulcers (92% diabetic) that had stalled after 4+ weeks of standard care or other products. All 9 wounds treated for closure closed within 8 weeks — mean 4.1 weeks, mean 3.3 reapplications, 94% mean area closure at 4 weeks. The other 3 cases used APIS® for wound-bed preparation or epithelialization over exposed tendon. No treatment-site infections in any of the 12 cases.

FAQ

Common questions.

What is APIS®'s regulatory status? +
APIS® is an FDA-cleared wound management device — SweetBio's materials state it plainly, and the 2023 Wounds publication cites FDA 510(k) 182725. Clearance is not the same as approval, and we use only the former. APIS® is prescription-only: federal (U.S.A.) law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a licensed healthcare practitioner.
Does APIS® have to be removed at dressing changes? +
No. APIS® is biodegradable and completely absorbable, so removal is not necessary — change the secondary dressing as needed, inspect the wound, and reapply APIS® as required. Per the IFU, do not forcibly remove parts of the sheet that have adhered to the wound.
Which patients should not receive APIS®? +
Patients with sensitivity to collagen and its derivatives, to porcine-derived products, or to honey. APIS® is also not indicated for third-degree burns, wound sites with infection or contamination, control of bleeding, or children and infants under 10 kg / 22 lbs — and it should not be applied until bleeding, infection, excessive exudate, and acute swelling are controlled.
Does the evidence actually show faster healing? +
It depends on the study, and we present each on its own terms. In the retrospective 47-wound comparison, wounds that closed did so in half the time versus standard of care (7.4 vs 14.8 weeks, P < .05), though most wounds in both groups did not close. In the 12-patient case series, all 9 closure-intent wounds closed within 8 weeks (mean 4.1). The Vanderbilt RCT found no significant difference in time to re-epithelialization — noninferiority, with significantly less pain and reduced skin thickness. We share the publications with prospective accounts.
What HCPCS code applies to APIS®? +
SweetBio's site lists APIS® under HCPCS A2010 (“Weekly CTP / skin substitute,” verified August 2026); the 2023 Wounds publication additionally cites A6021 (collagen dressing) and describes APIS® as CMS-covered. HCPCS assignments and payer policies change over time, so we confirm current coding at the time of order — Albacete MedDev provides coding guidance and documentation support, and never promises coverage or payment.
