The Context

The wound stalled. Then the billing rules changed.

Two separate problems land on the same clinic at the same time. A chronic wound that has not moved under standard care, and a 2026 skin-substitute billing environment that no longer tolerates loose documentation or billed wastage. APIS® is positioned against both.

Documentation threshold

≥ 4 weeks

SweetBio’s documentation guidance defines the chronic wound at four weeks or more, alongside documented failure of standard of care. That pairing — duration plus failed conservative management — is the spine of a defensible chart.

2026 billing reality

No wastage

As of 2026, CMS no longer accepts wastage modifiers, and providers are to bill usage for the exact size of the wound. JW and JZ modifiers are not permitted for skin substitutes. Only the applied portion is billed. Discarded product is not.

Operational fit

One sheet

Supplied as an all-in-one sheet that is ready to apply in minutes, trims or folds to the defect, and conforms to the wound bed. It resorbs as the wound progresses — removal is not necessary, so no application creates a removal appointment.

Documentation thresholds above are SweetBio’s stated best practices, not a coverage guarantee. Coverage, medical necessity, and payment are determined by the payer. Coding and reimbursement decisions are the responsibility of the provider and subject to payer policies.

Composition

Three known ingredients. One engineered sheet.

APIS® is a wound management device comprised of a highly purified collagen derivative (gelatin), Manuka honey, and hydroxyapatite. SweetBio’s core technology, MX™, is what binds those materials into a single conformable sheet rather than a layered stack.

01 · Matrix

Collagen derivative

A highly purified collagen derivative — gelatin, porcine-derived. Described by the manufacturer as a natural extracellular matrix protein beneficial for cell migration, adhesion, and proliferation.

02 · Honey

Manuka honey

100% UMF-certified New Zealand Manuka honey, known for antibacterial and anti-inflammatory effects. Note the contraindication list below: APIS® is not indicated for infected or contaminated wound sites.

03 · Mineral

Hydroxyapatite

A naturally occurring mineral known to bind proteins and, per the manufacturer’s cited literature, to promote healing via angiogenesis.

04 · MX™

The synthesis

MX™ is SweetBio’s core technology — the deliberate reconstruction of those natural elements into one biomaterial designed around the demands of the healing process rather than around a single stage of it.

What the device is intended to do

The intended use stated in the Instructions for Use is specific and worth quoting to a payer verbatim: to cover and protect the wound, absorb wound exudate, and provide and maintain a moist wound environment.

  • Cover & protectAn all-in-one sheet placed directly onto the prepared wound bed and secured with a secondary dressing
  • Absorb exudateFor highly exudating wounds, pre-hydration may not be needed at all — the sheet takes up wound fluid
  • Maintain moistureProvides and maintains a moist wound environment across the dressing-change interval
  • ConformTrims or folds to the defect and naturally conforms to the wound bed; may expand slightly in length and width on hydration
  • ResorbBiodegradable and completely absorbable — removal is not necessary, and adhered portions are never forcibly removed
  • Apply throughoutCan be applied from the onset of the wound and for its duration, reapplied as required at reassessment

Engineered across the closure cascade

SweetBio positions APIS® as supporting wound closure from the early stages of bacteria and inflammation through the late stages of proliferation and remodeling. In the manufacturer’s own literature that specific statement is cited only to references 5–7 — the bench and comparison papers. The clinical publications are cited to a separate claim. Read the cascade positioning as mechanism, not as clinical outcome.

  • ClinicalLower-extremity chronic wound experience, preliminary clinical evaluation, secondary intention healing, and a randomized pilot study in Mohs defects
  • MechanisticTwo explicitly in vitro — bacteria reduction capability, and inflammatory-marker and growth-factor secretion analysis — plus a preliminary direct comparison of inflammatory reduction and growth-factor production capability across three commercially available wound products
  • Not a claim of sterility of the woundIn vitro antibacterial capability is bench data. APIS® remains contraindicated in infection or contamination at the wound site

Required qualifier on the closure and pain claims

SweetBio’s 4–8 week wound closure and lower patient-reported pain in Mohs wounds statements carry this footnote in the manufacturer’s own literature, and it travels with them here: Based on clinical and in vitro data; claims have not been cleared by the FDA. Faster wound closure reported in slower healing and chronic wounds compared to standard of care.

APIS® is cleared through the FDA 510(k) pathway — cleared, not approved — for the management of wounds as listed in the indications. The closure-rate and pain statements sit outside that clearance.

Application Workflow

Five steps, per the IFU.

APIS® can be applied from the onset of the wound and for its duration. Follow the most recent procedures and best practices for wound management for preparation, application, and secondary dressing.

Step 01

Prepare the wound bed

Prepare per standard wound care protocols and debride if necessary. Do not apply until bleeding, infection, excessive exudate, and acute swelling are controlled.

Step 02

Select size and count

Measure the wound and select the appropriate size and number of APIS® devices. Maximum weekly usage is size-specific and differs for adults and children — the table is below.

Step 03

Hydrate, trim, place

Low or no exudate: hydrate both sides with sterile saline for up to 2 minutes, or until fully hydrated — it may expand slightly. Highly exudating: hydration may not be needed. Cut or fold to fit, then place directly onto the wound.

Step 04

Secondary dressing

Cover and secure with a suitable secondary dressing to ensure adherence to the wound and to provide protection. APIS® is not a standalone dressing system.

Step 05

Reassess and reapply

Change the secondary dressing as needed per protocol and exudate level. Inspect for healing, adverse reactions, or infection and reapply APIS® as required. Removal is unnecessary — never forcibly remove adhered portions.

Condensed from APIS® Instructions for Use, IFU-011 rev2. This page is a summary for evaluation and does not replace the IFU. Read the full Instructions for Use, which accompany every unit, before first application.

Precautions and adverse reactions

Because of its composition, individuals with known sensitivity to collagen, porcine-derived materials, or honey may experience an allergic reaction. Remove APIS® and discontinue use if signs of sensitivity appear.

Do not apply until bleeding, infection, excessive exudate, and acute swelling are controlled. Contact a healthcare professional if signs of infection occur (for example increased pain), if wound color and/or odor changes, if the wound does not begin to show signs of healing, or if any other unexpected symptoms occur.

Federal (U.S.A.) law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a licensed healthcare practitioner. Four mechanics, straight from SweetBio’s 2026 reimbursement guide: use place of service 11; bill the CPT code or codes plus A2010 for the total square centimeters applied; combine wounds by anatomical location; and make sure every add-on code accompanies its primary.

Documentation best practices

Seven elements. Every one of them is something an auditor looks for, and every one of them is easier to capture at the visit than to reconstruct months or years later during a records request.

  • ChronicityChronic wound ≥ 4 weeks

  • Product size appliedWhich SKU, how much applied, how much discarded

Common questions

What clinicians ask about APIS®.

What exactly is APIS® made of? +
A wound management device comprised of a highly purified collagen derivative (gelatin), Manuka honey, and hydroxyapatite. The collagen derivative is porcine-derived and described as a natural extracellular matrix protein beneficial for cell migration, adhesion, and proliferation. The honey is 100% UMF-certified New Zealand Manuka, known for antibacterial and anti-inflammatory effects. Hydroxyapatite is a naturally occurring mineral known to bind proteins and, per the cited literature, to promote healing via angiogenesis. SweetBio’s MX™ technology is what combines them into a single conformable sheet.
Is APIS® FDA approved? +
No — it is FDA 510(k) cleared, which is a different thing and the distinction matters in a chart and in a claim. Clearance covers the intended use stated in the Instructions for Use: to cover and protect the wound, absorb wound exudate, and provide and maintain a moist wound environment, in the management of the listed wound types under the supervision of a healthcare professional. Marketing statements about 4–8 week closure and lower patient-reported pain in Mohs wounds sit outside that clearance and carry the manufacturer’s own footnote: based on clinical and in vitro data; claims have not been cleared by the FDA.
How strong is the clinical evidence, honestly? +
Mixed, and we would rather say so than have you find out from the reference list. There are four clinical publications — two lower-extremity ulcer evaluations (Rodriguez 2020, described by its authors as preliminary; Rodriguez 2023), a secondary-intention healing report (McMurray 2021), and a randomized controlled trial that its own authors call a pilot study in Mohs surgical defects (Arnaud 2023). There are three mechanistic papers: two explicitly in vitro (bacteria reduction capability in Williams 2021; inflammatory-marker and growth-factor secretion in Rodriguez 2021), plus a preliminary direct comparison of inflammatory reduction and growth-factor production capability across three commercially available wound products (Rodriguez 2022). The 4–8 week closure figure is the manufacturer’s summary across chronic and acute wounds and carries the FDA qualifier. Bench data is mechanism, not outcome, and we do not present it as outcome.
Can I use it on an infected wound? +
No. Infections or contamination in the wound site are a contraindication, and the precautions state that APIS® should not be applied until bleeding, infection, excessive exudate, and acute swelling are controlled. The in vitro antibacterial work on Manuka honey does not change that — it is bench data, not a clinical antimicrobial indication. The full contraindication list is: sensitivity to collagen and its derivatives, sensitivity to porcine-derived products, sensitivity to honey, third degree burns, infection or contamination in the wound site, control of bleeding, and children and infant patients under 10 kg / 22 lbs.
Does it need to be removed between applications? +
No. APIS® devices are biodegradable and completely absorbable, so removal is not necessary, and adhered portions should never be forcibly removed. The secondary dressing is changed as needed depending on wound type, exudate level, dressing type, patient condition, and patient movement, per your standard protocols. At each assessment, inspect the wound for healing or for signs of adverse reaction, infection, or other complications, and reapply APIS® as required.
How many sheets can I use in a week? +
The IFU states a maximum weekly usage per size. For adults: 44 of the 1.6 × 1.6 cm (2.56 cm²), 18 of the 2.5 × 2.5 cm (6.25 cm²), 7 of the 4.0 × 4.0 cm (16.0 cm²), 4 of the 5.0 × 5.0 cm (25.0 cm²), and 2 of the 6.0 × 8.0 cm (48.0 cm²). For children above 10 kg / 22 lbs the corresponding figures are 23, 9, 3, 2, and 1. These are ceilings, not treatment plans — the diabetic heel ulcer case SweetBio publishes used four applications across five visits in five weeks.
How is it coded, and what changed for 2026? +
The product is billed under HCPCS A2010 — APIS, per square centimeter, with the application billed under the CPT 15271–15278 families. In the physician office, use place of service 11, bill the CPT code or codes plus A2010 for the total square centimeters applied, combine wounds by anatomical location, and pair every add-on code with its primary. What changed: as of 2026, CMS no longer accepts wastage modifiers, providers bill usage for the exact size of the wound, and JW and JZ modifiers are not permitted for skin substitutes. Only the applied portion is billed; discarded product is documented but not charged.
