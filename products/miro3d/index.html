ACELLULAR PORCINE LIVER MATRIX  ·  HCPCS A2025

Miro3D® Three-dimensional wound matrix

A 2 cm thick, open-porous acellular scaffold derived from perfusion-decellularized porcine liver, trimmed at the chair side to fill a wound and hold contact with its walls and base. FDA 510(k) cleared under K221520. Coded under HCPCS A2025 — per cubic centimeter: by the volume placed, not the surface covered.

The Problem

Some wounds are not a surface. They are a space.

Length and width are what a flat graft answers. Depth is the dimension it cannot. A thin sheet laid over a cavity bridges the opening; the walls and base of the defect never see the graft at all.

The bridging problem

A sheet spans the rim

Placed over a cavity, a thin sheet graft makes contact where the wound is shallowest — the rim. The labeling directs placement in maximum possible contact with healthy, well-vascularized tissue — walls and base, and those are exactly what a flat construct leaves untouched.

The construct

A 2 cm volume

Every Miro3D SKU is its footprint × 2 cm. Where a flat sheet bridges over a cavity, the 2 cm construct is trimmed to sit inside it — in contact with the walls and base, so host tissue can grow into the open scaffold.

The coding consequence

Coded as a volume

Miro3D is coded under HCPCS A2025, per cubic centimeter — by the volume placed, not the surface covered. The unit of the code matches the unit of the product, which is why depth belongs in the measurement and in the note.

The manufacturer’s own framing: “unlike thin grafts, Miro3D provides volume in deep wounds.” Product selection, wound assessment and the decision to use any wound matrix remain with the treating clinician.

How It Is Made

An organ’s architecture, with the cells removed.

Miro3D is derived from porcine liver — chosen because the liver is a highly vascularized organ, so its extracellular matrix is a naturally open, interconnected three-dimensional network rather than a flat dermal plane.

01 · Perfusion

Decellularized from within

Decellularizing solution is delivered through the native vasculature, reaching the whole organ from the inside instead of soaking it from the surface.

02 · Scaffold

Acellular collagen matrix

Cells are removed; the non-crosslinked collagen matrix and its open channel structure remain intact. The 510(k) file describes the result as an animal-sourced, non-crosslinked, acellular collagen tissue matrix.

03 · Formed

Dried, cut & sterilized

Processed, dried and cut into ten sizes, each 2 cm thick. Packaged dry and terminally sterilized in its packaging by electron-beam irradiation, to a sterility assurance level of 10−6.

What the construct is designed to do

  • Occupy the defectAt 2 cm thick it is a volume, not a film. The manufacturer’s framing is that unlike thin grafts, Miro3D provides volume in deep wounds.
  • Hold appositionTrimmed to shape, it is intended to sit in “maximum possible contact with healthy, well-vascularized tissue” — walls and base, not just the rim.
  • Stay open and porousDescribed in the labeling as an open, porous acellular graft that provides a protective environment for wound management.
  • Integrate, not be stripped outAs healing proceeds, loose edges may be trimmed away — but the IFU is explicit: do not remove Miro3D that has integrated.
  • ResorbThe 510(k) comparison table lists the device as resorbable, the same as its predicate.

What it is not

Miro3D is a 510(k)-cleared wound matrix — not a permanent skin replacement, not an implant, and not a substitute for systemic antimicrobial therapy. It does not replace debridement, offloading, compression, revascularization or infection control; it is placed into a wound bed those things have already made ready.

Chair-Side Preparation

Five steps, one clinic visit.

No freezer and no thaw. The construct arrives dry at room temperature and is prepared in the room with scissors, a basin and saline.

Step 01

Prepare the wound

Standard technique, free of debris and devitalized tissue. Initial debridement may be needed so the wound edges contain viable tissue. Measure length × width × depth.

Step 02

Trim to the defect

Using aseptic technique, cut the matrix with scissors or a scalpel to the size and shape that gives maximum contact with healthy tissue. It is 2 cm thick as supplied.

Step 03

Hydrate ≥ 5 minutes

Room-temperature sterile saline or Lactated Ringer’s. Replace the snap-on lid — the material is buoyant and must stay submerged for the full soak.

Step 04

Place within 4 hours

Position in contact with the wound walls and base, within four hours of hydration. Secure with the clinician’s preferred fixation if desired.

Step 05

Dress in two layers

A non-adherent primary dressing over the matrix, then a secondary dressing to manage exudate and keep the construct moist and in place.

Handling detail that matters

  • Trim before you soakThe construct is cut to the shape of the defect using aseptic technique; hydration follows trimming.
  • Keep it underDry matrix floats. The lid goes back on so the whole construct stays submerged for the full five minutes — a partially soaked block does not seat.
  • Contact, not coverageThe goal is maximum possible contact with healthy, well-vascularized tissue — walls and base, not just the rim.
  • At follow-upLoose edges may be trimmed away as healing proceeds. Miro3D that has integrated should not be removed.
  • Storage between casesRoom temperature, dry, in the original package. The 510(k) summary states no special storage conditions are required.

Longer soak than the flat predicate. The 510(k) comparison table lists a minimum five-minute rehydration for Miro3D against two minutes for the wet, compressed MiroDerm sheet. Build the soak into room turnover rather than rushing it.

Choosing a size

The range splits into two footprint shapes. Four are square — 2×2, 3×3, 4×4 and 5×5 cm. Six are rectangular — 5×2, 10×2, 6×5, 7×5, 8×5 and 10×5 cm, the last four all 5 cm on the short side. Selection is the treating clinician’s call; the geometry is simply what is on the shelf.

  • Trim down, not upVolume is as supplied; the matrix is cut to the shape of the defect. The closer the footprint starts to the defect, the less is trimmed away.

The substantial-equivalence argument

Miro3D was cleared as the dry, uncompressed version of MiroDerm, which is supplied as a wet, compressed flat sheet. FDA accepted that the change in configuration does not change the intended use, chemical composition or fundamental scientific technology of the collagen matrix. Same material, same product code, same principle of operation — a protective environment for wound healing.

  • Configuration at clearanceThe 510(k) summary describes four sizes (2×2, 3×3, 5×5 and 10×5 cm, all 2 cm thick); the marketed range is now ten. The predicate is a flat sheet in nine sizes, 0.3 to 1.5 mm thick.
  • BiocompatibilityAll applicable endpoints per ISO 10993-1:2018 — cytotoxicity, sensitization, intracutaneous reactivity, acute/subacute/subchronic systemic toxicity, material-mediated pyrogenicity, implantation and genotoxicity.
  • Viral inactivationThe manufacturing process is stated to be capable of inactivating four viruses.
  • Bench testingResidual DNA, collagen analysis and denaturation temperature, endotoxin, residual detergent, dimensions, rehydration, expiration dating and package stability.
  • MR statusThe K221520 summary states MR compatibility was not evaluated in that submission. MR Safe labeling is carried under the separate clearance K223257, 21 Nov 2022.

Evidence honesty — the wound-healing data is preclinical

The only wound-healing study in the K221520 file is a GLP-compliant animal study comparing Miro3D to MiroDerm as a control article in a porcine full-thickness wound healing model. The reported result is that Miro3D performed comparably to MiroDerm in all aspects evaluated. That is an animal model, not clinical proof, and it is a comparison against the predicate rather than against standard care. We will not represent it as anything else.

A follow-up FDA letter dated 25 August 2022 was an administrative correction that added the 510(k) Summary document to the 18 August 2022 substantial-equivalence letter; the submission was not re-reviewed.

Coding & Reimbursement

HCPCS A2025 — per cubic centimeter.

The code’s unit of measure is a cubic centimeter, not a square one — the same unit the product is manufactured and supplied in. Billing follows the volume placed, not the surface covered.

What that changes in the note

A per-square-centimeter product invites a two-dimensional note. A per-cubic-centimeter product does not. Depth stops being a descriptive adjective and becomes part of the arithmetic that supports the units billed.

  • Record all three dimensionsLength, width and depth at each visit — the measurement the code’s unit rests on.
  • Volume placed, not volume openedThe matrix is trimmed to the shape of the defect, so what goes into the wound is routinely less than the volume as supplied. Document what was placed and what was discarded.
  • Applied units onlyCMS has stated that, effective 1 January 2026, the JW and JZ modifiers no longer apply to skin substitute products that are not regulated as drugs or biologics, and that discarded units of those products are not billable to Medicare. Do not report JW or JZ for A2025; bill only the units actually applied and documented.
  • Size selection is part of the recordWhich model was opened, and why that footprint suited the defect, belongs in the note alongside the measurement it came from.
  • Product identity in the chartModel number, size, volume and GTIN are all on the box — capture them with the lot and expiration.

The Rest of the Platform

One platform. Three other shapes.

Not every defect takes a trimmed block. Reprise supplies three additional presentations alongside Miro3D, each with its own HCPCS code and its own labeling.

HCPCS A2029

MiroTract®

Guidewire-delivered and radially compressed for tunneled and undermined wounds, expanding once hydrated. Four sizes, 8–19 cm³. For the tract you can probe but cannot open.

HCPCS A2030

Miro3D® Fibers

The same matrix in particulate form, for irregular and contoured defects where a cut block will not seat cleanly. Four sizes, 100–700 mg.

HCPCS A2031

MiroDry®

A thin sheet presentation, 6.5 mm average thickness — for the shallow, broad wounds where a 2 cm volume is the wrong tool and a surface is genuinely a surface.
