3D ACELLULAR WOUND MATRIX · HCPCS A2025

Miro3D® fills the wound’s third dimension.

A sterile, non-crosslinked acellular collagen scaffold derived from perfusion-decellularized porcine liver, by Reprise Biomedical, Inc. Every size is 2 cm thick — trimmed at the chair side to occupy deep, tunneled and irregular wounds instead of bridging over them. Coded under HCPCS A2025, per cubic centimeter.

The construct

Some wounds are not a surface. They are a space.

Miro3D® is built for the wound that has depth. It is an open, porous scaffold supplied as a 2 cm thick block — a volume, not a film — that provides a protective environment for wound management.

2 cm thick

Volume, not film

Every SKU is its footprint × 2 cm. Where a thin sheet bridges a cavity, Miro3D® is cut to sit inside it — “unlike thin grafts, Miro3D provides volume in deep wounds,” as Reprise puts it. The manufacturer’s design intent is specific: cut to desired size for full wall apposition and conformity to the wound bed, in deep, tunneling and irregular wounds.

Porcine liver

Perfusion decellularization

The source organ is what Reprise calls the highly vascularized porcine liver — an organ whose extracellular matrix is a naturally open, interconnected 3D network rather than a flat dermal plane. Decellularizing solution is perfused through the organ’s own vascular tree, clearing cellular material while the non-crosslinked collagen architecture stays standing. The scaffold is then dried, cut to size and terminally sterilized by e-beam.

Predicate K140510

MiroDerm heritage

Miro3D® is the dry, uncompressed configuration of MiroDerm, its 510(k) predicate — same source material, chemical composition, principle of operation and sterilization, per the 510(k) summary. In a GLP animal study of porcine full-thickness wounds, Miro3D performed comparably to MiroDerm in all aspects evaluated. FDA found the devices substantially equivalent on August 18, 2022.

Indications

Cleared indications, stated verbatim.

The intended-use statement below is quoted from the FDA 510(k) summary (K221520). The clinical-use list beside it is how Reprise describes where the matrix is reached for in practice.

Intended use — per K221520

“The Miro3D Wound Matrix is intended for the management of wounds including:”

  • Partial and full thickness wounds
  • Pressure ulcers
  • Venous ulcers
  • Chronic vascular ulcers
  • Diabetic ulcers
  • Tunneled, undermined wounds
  • Trauma wounds (abrasions, lacerations, second-degree burns, and skin tears)
  • Draining wounds
  • Surgical wounds (donor sites/grafts, post-Mohs’ surgery, post-laser surgery, podiatric, wound dehiscence)

Current manufacturer materials phrase the burn indication as “partial thickness burns.” Federal (USA) law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a physician. The FDA-cleared Instructions for Use govern; this page is not a substitute for them.

Clinical uses — per Reprise

Reprise lists a “broad range of clinical uses” for the matrix:

  • Stage III and IV pressure ulcers
  • Diabetic foot ulcers
  • Necrotizing fasciitis
  • Wound dehiscence
  • Perirectal abscess
  • Pilonidal sinus disease
  • Partial and full-thickness wounds
  • Trauma wounds
  • Other tunneled and undermined wounds

Boundaries, plainly: Miro3D® is a 510(k)-cleared wound matrix — not a permanent skin replacement, not an implant, and not a substitute for systemic antimicrobial therapy. It is contraindicated in patients with known sensitivity to porcine material, is not indicated for third-degree burns, and should not be applied until excessive exudate, bleeding and acute swelling are controlled and infection has been cleared.

In practice

Trimmed to the defect, placed to the walls.

Preparation is chair-side and unspecialized: scissors or a scalpel, saline, five minutes. The handling parameters below are drawn from the manufacturer’s IFU-based materials.

Five steps at the chair side

The sequence, condensed from Reprise’s product literature:

  • Prepare the wound. Standard technique, free of debris and devitalized tissue; debride so the edges contain viable tissue.
  • Trim to the defect. Cut the matrix to the size and shape that gives maximum contact with healthy, well-vascularized tissue — walls and base, not just the rim.
  • Hydrate at least five minutes in room-temperature sterile saline or lactated Ringer’s. Hydrated, the matrix softens slightly and conforms to the wound bed.
  • Fill the space. Place within four hours of hydration, in contact with the wound walls and base; secure if desired.
  • Dress in two layers. A non-adherent primary dressing over the matrix, then a secondary dressing to manage exudate and keep the construct moist and in place.

As healing proceeds, loose edges may be trimmed away — but the IFU is explicit that Miro3D® which has integrated into the wound should not be removed. The scaffold is resorbable; host tissue grows into it.

Clinical evidence

The evidence, at its true weight.

We state what exists rather than round it up: an animal study behind the clearance, a case-based human record, and randomized trials that are enrolling — not yet reported.

GLP animal study

Behind the clearance

Reprise sponsored a GLP-compliant animal study comparing Miro3D against MiroDerm as control in a porcine full-thickness wound healing model. Per the 510(k) summary, Miro3D performed comparably in all aspects evaluated. That supported substantial equivalence — it is animal data, not evidence of human clinical outcomes.

Case-based human record

Case series & posters

The published human record is observational: a 2024 retrospective case series in deep, tunneling or irregular diabetic foot wounds (11 patients, 13 wounds, single center, no control arm — 7 of 13 closed by 12 weeks; all 13 eventually closed, mean therapy 13.1 weeks), a single-case report, and manufacturer case studies plus SAWC posters and presentations (2023–2025) spanning dehiscence, necrotizing fasciitis, abscess, pilonidal and amputation wounds.

Enrolling now

No RCT results yet

No randomized or comparative results have been published. Four registered trials are enrolling — including a 180-patient, 14-site randomized controlled trial in chronic diabetic foot ulcers — and none has reported. Reprise’s own caveat on its case material: results “may not be representative of all potential clinical outcomes,” and “further study is necessary to determine the benefit of Miro3D in a variety of wound applications.”

Reimbursement

Billed by the cubic centimeter, not the square.

CMS assigned HCPCS Level II code A2025 — Miro3D, per cubic centimeter — effective October 1, 2023. Key coding facts below are drawn from Reprise’s 2026 Coding and Billing Guide (SM-00193 Rev. M). Codes describe billing, not endorsement — and never guarantee payment.

The unit

Per cm³ applied

One billing unit is one cubic centimeter of matrix actually applied and documented in the record — not the volume opened. Effective January 1, 2026, JW/JZ modifiers no longer apply to non-BLA skin substitutes, and CMS states discarded units may not be billed to Medicare under any circumstances. The application itself is coded separately under CPT 15271–15278, which count square centimeters — the two unit counts differ by design.

2026 rules

Unpackaged product payment

Under the CY 2026 PFS and OPPS final rules, CMS unpackages skin-substitute products from payment for their application. In hospital outpatient and ASC settings, A2025 links to APC 6001 (510(k) skin substitute products) with status indicator S1 for separate product payment. In the office, document Miro3D use in Box 19 of the CMS-1500. Typical places of service: office (11), home (12), inpatient (21), outpatient (22), ASC (24), SNF (31), nursing facility (32); inpatient stays fall under MS-DRGs.

Coverage

MAC by MAC, documented

The national skin-substitute LCDs slated for January 1, 2026 were withdrawn on December 24, 2025, so coverage is governed by local MAC policies and varies by jurisdiction; the CMS WISeR model (2026–2031) may require prior authorization in NJ, OH, OK and TX. Claims must be medically necessary and supported by documentation. Albacete MedDev provides coding and documentation support — sample claim forms and a letter of medical necessity exist in the manufacturer’s guide — and makes no promise of coverage or payment.

Common questions

What to know about Miro3D®.

Is Miro3D FDA approved? +
No — it is FDA 510(k)-cleared, which is a different determination. On August 18, 2022, FDA found Miro3D® substantially equivalent (K221520) to its predicate, the MiroDerm Wound Matrix (K140510). It is an unclassified pre-amendment device, product code KGN, and a follow-on clearance (K223257, November 2022) supports its MR Safe labeling. Clearance permits legal marketing for the stated indications; it is not a premarket approval, and we never describe it as one.
How is Miro3D billed under HCPCS A2025? +
A2025 — Miro3D, per cubic centimeter — took effect October 1, 2023. Bill the cubic centimeters actually applied and documented; supplied volumes run 8 to 100 cm³ across ten sizes. From January 1, 2026, JW/JZ modifiers do not apply and discarded units may not be billed to Medicare. The application is reported separately under CPT 15271–15278, which are counted per square centimeter — the product and procedure units are not meant to match. Coverage and payment vary by payer and MAC; Albacete MedDev provides coding and documentation support, not coverage guarantees.
How does Miro3D relate to MiroDerm? +
Miro3D® is the dry, uncompressed configuration of MiroDerm, which is supplied as a wet, compressed flat sheet. Per the 510(k) summary, the two share the same material — perfusion-decellularized porcine liver — and the same chemical composition, principle of operation, biocompatibility and sterilization method. The configuration is the point: a 2 cm thick scaffold for wounds with depth, rather than a thin sheet for surfaces.
What clinical evidence supports Miro3D? +
Stated at its true weight: a GLP animal study (porcine full-thickness wounds, MiroDerm as control) supported the clearance, and the human record is case-based — a 2024 retrospective case series of 11 patients and 13 deep or tunneling diabetic foot wounds (single center, no control arm), a single-case report, and manufacturer case studies and SAWC conference posters. No randomized results have been published; four registered trials are enrolling, including a 180-patient multicenter RCT. Reprise itself notes the case experience “may not be representative of all potential clinical outcomes” and that further study is necessary.
How is Miro3D stored and prepared? +
Storage is unglamorous on purpose: room temperature, dry, in the original package, avoiding prolonged elevated temperatures — no refrigeration or special conditions. At the chair side, trim with scissors or a scalpel, rehydrate at least five minutes in room-temperature sterile saline or lactated Ringer’s (45 mL for the two smallest sizes, 100 mL for the rest), keep the buoyant matrix submerged, and place within four hours of hydration. Sterile, single use; do not resterilize or reuse. MR Safe per manufacturer labeling.
