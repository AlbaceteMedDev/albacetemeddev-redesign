Composition

Connective tissue matrix — and nothing else.

Interfyl is built as pure extracellular-matrix scaffold: no amnion, no cells or cellular debris, no antibiotic residuals. What remains is the structural chemistry the integument is made of.

Chorionic plate

The chorion difference

Per the Interfyl product brochure (BIONX Surgical, 2026), Interfyl is the only CTM filler derived exclusively from the chorionic plate of the human placenta. It contains no amnion — only connective tissue matrix, intended for homologous use: the replacement or supplementation of damaged or inadequate integumental tissue.

Decellularized

No cells. No cellular debris.

Processing is completely decellularizing: no DNA, growth factors, or other cytokines remain in appreciable amounts. And because no antibiotics are used in processing, there are no antibiotic residuals — the product brochure notes Interfyl can be used in antibiotic-sensitive individuals.

Native ECM

Structure the skin recognizes

Minimal manipulation retains the fundamental structural and functional characteristics of connective-tissue ECM: the major structural proteins collagen and elastin — just like skin — plus fibronectin, laminin, glycosaminoglycans, and proteoglycans. The matrix provides mechanical support and elasticity, the common functions of ECM in the integument.

Indications for use (package insert)

For surgical indications, Interfyl's labeled uses include, but are not limited to:

  • Treatment of soft tissue voids and correction of soft tissue defects
  • Soft tissue augmentation during repair of dehisced or complicated surgical closures
  • Repair of small surgical defects resulting from medical or surgical conditions — including those with exposed vital structures (bone, tendon, ligament, or nerve)

For wound indications:

  • Augmentation of deficient or inadequate soft tissue
  • Treatment of deep dermal wounds and surgical wounds
  • Soft tissue voids resulting from tunneling wounds, fistula tracts, or dermal undermining — including those with exposed vital structures

Per the product brochure, Interfyl can be used with acute injuries and/or chronic conditions, and the flowable form conforms to challenging contours — filling irregular spaces and soft-tissue deficits resulting from trauma or surgery.

What the cited data shows

One study is named across the product literature (the Arthrex/Celularity Patient's Guide): Pashuck ET, Mao Y, Kim K, John K, Smiell J, Bhatia MB — A human placenta-derived decellularized connective tissue matrix (CTM) supports cellular functions involved in wound healing processes, presented at the Symposium on Advanced Wound Care (SAWC) Fall, Las Vegas, NV. The literature cites it to support cell attachment: the scaffold provides a favorable environment for cells to attach, described as the first step in natural healing.

Graded plainly: that is a conference presentation at the mechanism level — cellular function, not clinical endpoints — and the citation reports no sample size. No comparative clinical trial appears in the source materials, so we make no outcome claims. Interfyl's case rests on its composition and labeled function: a decellularized connective-tissue ECM providing mechanical support and elasticity.

Formats & ordering

Six configurations. One code: Q4171.

Both formats ship in double-pouch packaging — the inner pouch and contents sterile — and store at ambient room temperature, ready to use.

Flowable

Prefilled syringe · 0.3, 0.6, 1, 1.5 mL

Each kit contains the dry product syringe, an empty syringe, a double-female luer-lock adapter, and a flexible 1.5-inch cannula. Mix 1:1 with sterile saline for a toothpaste-like consistency — or adjust the ratio to preference — with a minimum of 15 plunger passes for a homogeneous mix. An 18-gauge needle can be used if desired.

Particulate

Vial · 50 mg and 100 mg

A single vial, applied dry — tapped or sprinkled from the vial, or placed with non-traumatic forceps — or wetted to a paste-like consistency: add 0.3 mL sterile fluid to the 50 mg vial, 0.6 mL to the 100 mg. Consistency adjusts with more or less fluid.

FAQ

Common questions.

What is Interfyl's regulatory pathway? +

Interfyl is regulated under Section 361 of the Public Health Service Act as a human cell, tissue, and cellular and tissue-based product (HCT/P) per 21 CFR Part 1271.10(a). It is minimally manipulated during processing and marketed in accordance with FDA's requirements for HCT/Ps. That pathway is distinct from device clearance and drug approval — so neither of those terms applies to Interfyl, and we don't use them. It is distributed only to licensed health care practitioners.

What is the HCPCS code, and what does it get me? +

Q4171 (Interfyl, 1 mg) — a product-specific code. A code identifies the product for billing; it is not an endorsement and not a guarantee of coverage or payment, which are payer decisions. Albacete MedDev provides coding and documentation support to your claims team.

How is the flowable format prepared? +

Fill the included empty syringe with equal parts sterile saline or another sterile non-viscous fluid — the 1:1 product-to-liquid ratio gives a toothpaste-like consistency, adjustable with more or less fluid. Connect the syringes with the double-female luer lock, release the fluid into the product syringe, and push both plungers back and forth at least 15 times for a homogeneous mix. Apply through the included flexible 1.5-inch cannula, or an 18-gauge needle if desired. Once opened, use within two hours.

What are the storage and handling rules? +

Store in the original packaging in a clean, dry environment at ambient room temperature; shelf life is 10 years, and product must be used before the expiration date on the pouch. Contents are sterile while the container is unopened and undamaged — never resterilize. Once opened, use within two hours or discard. Each syringe or vial serves a single patient on one occasion. Do not use in clinically infected sites, and never for intravenous, intra-arterial, intra-ocular, or intrathecal applications.

Does Interfyl contain amnion, cells, or antibiotics? +

No — per the product brochure, Interfyl is derived exclusively from the chorionic plate and contains no amnion, only connective tissue matrix. It is completely decellularized: no cells or cellular debris, and no DNA, growth factors, or other cytokines in appreciable amounts. No antibiotics are used in processing, so there are no antibiotic residuals — which is why the product brochure notes it can be used in antibiotic-sensitive individuals.
