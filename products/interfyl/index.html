The Problem

Tissue deficits have depth.

Damage that follows trauma or surgery does not arrive as a flat plane. Interfyl is designed to fill voids and to replace or supplement damaged or inadequate integumental tissue — and its flowable form is intended to conform to the space it is placed into.

Geometry

Voids, not planes

Interfyl’s flowable form enables it to conform to challenging contours, filling irregular spaces or soft tissue deficits resulting from trauma or surgery.

Indication

Damaged or inadequate tissue

Interfyl is intended for use as the replacement or supplementation of damaged or inadequate integumental tissue. That is the stated intended use — reproduced plainly, without extension.

Handling & Preparation

Four checkpoints, and a two-hour clock.

The handling rules for Interfyl are short, and two of them are hard stops. Brief the whole team on the two-hour open window and the single-patient rule before the first case, not during it.

Step 01

Inspect

Always handle Interfyl using aseptic techniques. Inspect all packaging integrity. Do not use if the package seal is broken or the container has been violated. Discard material if mishandling has caused possible damage or contamination. Use before the expiration date on the product pouch.

Step 02

Reconstitute the flowable

Sterile solution is supplied with the flowable presentations. In the three sizes covered by the sizing study the volume provided is 0.3 mL, 0.6 mL and 1 mL respectively. In the bench method, 0.9% saline is collected in the top syringe, mixed with Interfyl in the bottom syringe, and the pair is “pumped” repeatedly to encourage the powder into solution and reach a homogeneous consistency.

Step 03

Fill the deficit

Apply to the site. The flowable form is intended to conform to challenging contours, filling irregular spaces or soft tissue deficits. Interfyl should not be used in clinically infected sites.

Step 04

Close out

Once opened, Interfyl must be used within two hours or discarded per institutional procedures. Each syringe/vial is intended for use on a single patient on one occasion; discard any unused material per institutional procedures.

The mixing sequence in Step 02 is the procedure described in the internal bench-top surface-area study; it is reproduced here because it is the only preparation detail the public sizing guide publishes. It is not a substitute for the Interfyl package insert. Follow the package insert and your institutional protocol for clinical preparation. These recommendations are designed to serve only as general guidelines. They are not intended to supersede institutional protocols or professional clinical judgment concerning patient care.

How the estimate was produced

Worth reading before you quote a number to a colleague. The method is transparent, and its limits are visible from the method itself.

  • PreparationInterfyl flowable was mixed with the suggested volume of saline for the Interfyl weight — 0.9% saline in the top syringe, Interfyl in the bottom, pumped repeatedly to a homogeneous consistency.
  • ApplicationThe syringes were separated, the syringe tip excluded, and the solution applied to a glass slide, then compressed with a second glass slide.
  • Scale calibrationA 1 cm² square of grid paper was outlined in ImageJ and analyzed for pixel count — which accounts for the different visual scale depending on the height the image was taken.
  • Area measurementThe Interfyl area was outlined by circular estimate or hand sketch, analyzed for pixel count, and divided by the 1 cm² scale to estimate area in cm².
  • OutcomeAn average of 6 mg/cm² across all sizes tested.

Do not extrapolate. The study covers three flowable sizes. The 1.5 mL flowable and both particulate vials are outside it, and we will not multiply 6 mg/cm² out to fill the gap for you. Ask us and we will put the question to BIONX Surgical and come back to you in writing.

Regulatory status — read this before you write it into a policy

Interfyl is human placental tissue intended for homologous use. Human tissue products in this category are regulated as human tissue under 21 CFR Part 1271. Interfyl is not an FDA-approved drug and not a 510(k)-cleared device, so there is no clearance number to cite for it. Write “cleared” or “approved” into a policy document and the policy will be wrong.

Coding & Reimbursement

HCPCS Q4171 — and why a code is not coverage.

Interfyl® is billed under HCPCS Q4171. Worth knowing where that comes from: the code lives in the CMS HCPCS Level II code set, not in the manufacturer’s brochures, which carry no billing information at all. Codes, descriptors and unit definitions move on the CMS quarterly cycle, and a code implies nothing about whether a payer will cover the case. We confirm the current descriptor and the applicable policy with your MAC in writing before the first application. Four things to settle first.

Common questions

What to know about Interfyl® CTM.

What actually separates Interfyl from the rest of the placental category? +
Source layer. BIONX Surgical’s product literature describes Interfyl as the only CTM filler derived exclusively from the chorionic plate of the human placenta, and it does not contain amnion — it contains only connective tissue matrix. Interfyl is defined by a single source layer and by minimal processing, which helps retain the fundamental structure and functional characteristics of native connective tissue. That uniqueness statement is the supplier’s, reproduced from its literature; we present it as their claim rather than as an independent market survey.
Does Interfyl contain growth factors or living cells? +
No. There are no remaining cells or cellular debris in Interfyl, and consequently no DNA, growth factors, or other cytokines are present in appreciable amounts. What is present is the matrix: the major structural proteins collagen and elastin — just like the skin — plus fibronectin, laminin, glycosaminoglycans (GAGs) and proteoglycans. If your protocol depends on delivering growth factors or viable cells, this is not that product — the composition statement in the literature is explicit about what is absent. The case for Interfyl is structural.
Can I use it in a patient with antibiotic sensitivity? +
Yes — no antibiotics are used in Interfyl processing, so there are no antibiotic residuals, and it can be used in antibiotic-sensitive individuals. That is a distinct question from hypersensitivity to the product itself: Interfyl is contraindicated in patients with a known hypersensitivity to Interfyl, and if a recipient had an adverse reaction related to previous use, do not re-apply.
Flowable or particulate — how do I choose? +
Honestly: the public product material does not publish a selection algorithm, so we will not invent one. What it does say is that both forms exist “to meet a variety of surgical application needs,” and that the flowable form is what enables Interfyl to conform to challenging contours, filling irregular spaces or soft tissue deficits resulting from trauma or surgery. Note also that the coverage estimates we hold cover three flowable sizes only — neither particulate vial is in that study. Selection is a clinical judgment; bring us the case and we will get a specific answer from BIONX Surgical rather than guessing at one.
How much surface area does one syringe cover? +
From the internal bench-top study (data on file): HCTM030 — 0.3 mL, 40 mg of Interfyl with 0.3 mL of sterile solution — an estimated 7 cm². HCTM060 — 0.6 mL, 70 mg with 0.6 mL of solution — an estimated 12 cm². HCTM001 — the 100 mg flowable, with 1 mL of solution — an estimated 17 cm². The analysis averaged 6 mg/cm² across all sizes tested. These are bench-top figures measured by ImageJ pixel-area analysis on compressed glass slides, not clinical measurements, and actual coverage may vary based on wound characteristics, application technique, hydration and physician judgment. The 1.5 mL flowable and the particulate vials are not in that study, and we do not extrapolate a figure for them.
How was that coverage figure measured? +
Interfyl flowable was mixed with the suggested volume of saline for the Interfyl weight — 0.9% saline collected in the top syringe, mixed with Interfyl in the bottom syringe, then “pumped” repeatedly to bring the powder into solution at a homogeneous consistency. The syringes were separated, the tip excluded, and the solution applied to a glass slide and compressed with a second slide. ImageJ was then used to quantify the area: a 1 cm² square of grid paper was outlined and its pixel count recorded (which accounts for the different visual scale depending on the height the image was taken), the Interfyl area was outlined by circular estimate or hand sketch, and its pixel count was divided by the 1 cm² scale. It is a transparent method with visible limits — which is exactly why it should be read as a sizing guide rather than as evidence.
