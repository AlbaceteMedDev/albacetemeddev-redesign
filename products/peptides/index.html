Read this before the catalog — what “compounded” means

Every item on this page is a compounded drug preparation. Compounded drugs are not FDA-approved. FDA does not review compounded preparations for safety or efficacy before they are marketed, and no compounded preparation carries an FDA-approved label, an approved indication, or approved prescribing information.

That applies without exception to every molecule below — in both channels, in every presentation. It applies to compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide, which are not the branded products and must never be represented to a patient as though they were. It applies to the molecules with no FDA approval in any indication, and to the molecules whose evidence base is preclinical.

Prescribing, patient selection, monitoring and informed consent are the prescriber’s clinical and legal responsibility, governed by state pharmacy and medical-board rules as well as federal law. Albacete MedDev supplies the channel, the ordering workflow and the program support around it. We do not practice medicine and we do not compound.

“503A/B” is the supplier’s shorthand, not a regulatory designation

The supplier describes this channel as “503A/B.” That is the supplier’s shorthand. §503A and §503B are separate, mutually exclusive exemptions under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, and there is no hybrid registration. The shorthand indicates that preparations on this channel come through both a 503A compounding pharmacy and a 503B outsourcing facility.

Which exemption a given preparation is made under determines the rules that apply to it — whether it can be made without a named patient, whether your practice may hold it as stock, how it has to be labeled, and what quality standard it was produced to. Confirm the pathway per preparation when you order, and do not read the channel name as an answer to that question. What each exemption means is set out in the next section.

FDA registration of a 503B outsourcing facility is a registration, not an approval, and it does not make any preparation an approved drug. State pharmacy and medical-board rules govern what a practice may order, hold and administer, and they differ by state. Confirm your own state’s position before building a protocol around office stock.

The Regulatory Separation

What 503A and 503B actually mean.

This is the part of the page that matters most, so it is stated plainly and more than once. The two provisions describe how a preparation may lawfully be made — not whether it has been reviewed, approved, or proven. They are separate and mutually exclusive: a preparation is compounded under one of them or the other, never both, and no entity holds a combined “503A/B” registration.

What each one means

§503AA compounding pharmacy. Prepares a drug for an identified individual patient against a prescription. No office stock
§503BAn outsourcing facility. May compound without patient-specific prescriptions, which is what makes office stock possible at all
Mutually exclusiveA given preparation is made under one or the other. There is no hybrid pathway and no combined registration
Quality standard503B facilities produce under current good manufacturing practice (CGMP); 503A pharmacies compound to USP standards under state-board rules
FDA relationship503B facilities register with FDA; 503A pharmacies are licensed by the state board
Office-use labeling503B output compounded without a patient-specific prescription is labeled “Office Use Only” and “Not for resale”
ApprovalNeither pathway produces an FDA-approved drug product
Pre-market reviewNone — compounded drugs are not reviewed by FDA for safety or efficacy before marketing
LabelingNo approved label, no approved indication, no approved prescribing information
ResponsibilityThe prescriber’s — selection, consent, monitoring, documentation

What it changes in your practice

The distinction is operational before it is philosophical. It changes who the order is written for, where the box can go, and what your chart has to show.

  • One named patientA 503A order is written for one identified patient against a prescription. There is no anonymous office-stock model in that channel.
  • Stock without a nameA 503B outsourcing facility may compound without a patient-specific prescription — but your state board still governs what the practice may hold and administer.
  • Same disclosureBoth channels are compounded. Neither is FDA-approved. The consent conversation does not change from one side of the line to the other.
  • Not research chemicalsNothing in this catalog is supplied as research-use-only labeled material; every item is ordered through a licensed channel against a prescription. That describes what is supplied. It is not a finding that any particular preparation is lawful to compound — that question is settled per molecule, below.
  • Chart itRecord that the preparation is compounded, that it is not FDA-approved, what was discussed at consent, and the monitoring plan. That record is the defense.
  • Marketing is regulated tooHow a compounded preparation is advertised to patients is its own exposure. Do not borrow a branded product’s name, claims or trial data.

Said twice, on purpose. Compounded drugs are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety or efficacy before marketing. That is true of every molecule on this page, in both channels, in every presentation — the incretins included.

Bulk substance eligibility — the question that comes before the channel

A compounded preparation is lawful only if its active ingredient is an eligible bulk drug substance — one with a USP or NF monograph, one that is a component of an FDA-approved drug, or one on FDA’s §503A or §503B bulks list. Many peptides are on none of these lists, and FDA has categorized several as presenting significant safety risks.

Eligibility is decided per molecule, and it changes. A prescription and a licensed channel do not settle it: an ineligible substance does not become eligible because it was ordered correctly. Confirm current status for the specific molecule before you order it, and again before you build a protocol around it. Ask us and we will put the question to the pharmacy or the outsourcing facility in writing.

Tirzepatide

Dual GIP / GLP-1 receptor agonist
503AMyViaHealth

A single 39-amino-acid peptide that activates both the GIP and GLP-1 receptors. The dual mechanism is what separates it from the GLP-1-only agents — GIP agonism appears to contribute additional effects on appetite and on adipose insulin sensitivity.

Ordered for weight management and glycemic support protocols. Supplied compounded with methylcobalamin (B12) or glycine. A compounded preparation that is essentially a copy of a commercially available approved drug may not lawfully be supplied under FDCA §503A(b)(1)(D) or §503B(a)(5), and adding a component does not by itself create a permissible difference. A change is permissible only where the prescriber documents, for an identified patient, a clinical difference the approved product cannot meet. That determination and its documentation are the prescriber’s. Availability through either channel depends on current FDA shortage-list status and may change without notice.
