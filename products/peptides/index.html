Read this before the catalog — what “compounded” means

Every item on this page is a compounded drug preparation. Compounded drugs are not FDA-approved. FDA does not review compounded preparations for safety or efficacy before they are marketed, and no compounded preparation carries an FDA-approved label, an approved indication, or approved prescribing information.

That applies without exception to every molecule below — in both channels, in every presentation. It applies to compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide, which are not the branded products and must never be represented to a patient as though they were. It applies to the molecules with no FDA approval in any indication, and to the molecules whose evidence base is preclinical.

Prescribing, patient selection, monitoring and informed consent are the prescriber’s clinical and legal responsibility, governed by state pharmacy and medical-board rules as well as federal law. Albacete MedDev supplies the channel, the ordering workflow and the program support around it. We do not practise medicine and we do not compound.

The Regulatory Separation

What 503A and 503B actually mean.

This is the part of the page that matters most, so it is stated plainly and more than once. The two designations describe how a preparation may lawfully be made — not whether it has been reviewed, approved, or proven.

The statutory distinction

503AA compounding pharmacy. Prepares a drug for an identified individual patient against a prescription
503BAn outsourcing facility. May compound without patient-specific prescriptions
Quality standard503B facilities produce under current good manufacturing practice (CGMP)
FDA relationship503B facilities register with FDA; 503A pharmacies are licensed by the state board
ApprovalNeither pathway produces an FDA-approved drug product
Pre-market reviewNone — compounded drugs are not reviewed by FDA for safety or efficacy before marketing
LabellingNo approved label, no approved indication, no approved prescribing information
ResponsibilityThe prescriber’s — selection, consent, monitoring, documentation

What it changes in your practice

The distinction is operational before it is philosophical. It changes who the order is written for, where the box can go, and what your chart has to show.

  • One named patientA 503A order is written for one identified patient against a prescription. There is no anonymous office-stock model in that channel.
  • Stock without a nameA 503B outsourcing facility may compound without a patient-specific prescription — but your state board still governs what the practice may hold and administer.
  • Same disclosureBoth channels are compounded. Neither is FDA-approved. The consent conversation does not change from one side of the line to the other.
  • Not research chemicalsNothing in this catalog is supplied as research-use-only material. Every item comes through a licensed 503A or 503A/B channel, dispensed against a prescription.
  • Chart itRecord that the preparation is compounded, that it is not FDA-approved, what was discussed at consent, and the monitoring plan. That record is the defence.
  • Marketing is regulated tooHow a compounded preparation is advertised to patients is its own exposure. Do not borrow a branded product’s name, claims or trial data.

Said twice, on purpose. Compounded drugs are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety or efficacy before marketing. That is true of every molecule on this page, in both channels, in every presentation — the incretins included.

Getting Started

Four steps from licence to first shipment.

No distributor account, no minimum, no equipment. The channel is a prescriber account and an ordering portal.

Step 01

Credential

Provide your NPI or state licence number and the state you practise in. Both channels dispense to licensed prescribers only.

Step 02

Enrol in the channel

For 503A, sign up at mypharmacyorder.com and order through the portal. For 503A/B, we set you up on the MyViaHealth platform. Most practices run both.

Step 03

Prescribe

Write the prescription for an identified patient, document the compounded status and the consent conversation, and place the order in the channel that carries the molecule.

Step 04

Ship

To the clinic or direct to the patient, depending on channel and product. Testosterone ships direct to the patient only — never to the clinic.

What the cards deliberately do not say

There are no outcome promises here, and no dosing protocols. Mechanism is described where it is understood; where the supporting work is animal or in-vitro, the card says preclinical and stops.

  • No indicationsCompounded preparations have no approved indication. “Ordered for” describes clinical use, not a claim of benefit.
  • No dosingStrengths are listed because they are what is orderable. Dose, route and schedule are the prescriber’s judgement.
  • No comparisonsNo molecule here is presented as better than a branded product or than another molecule on the page.

Common questions

What providers ask about the peptide program.

Are any of these FDA-approved? +
No. Every item in this catalog is a compounded preparation, and compounded drugs are not FDA-approved. FDA does not review compounded preparations for safety or efficacy before they are marketed. A few molecules have an FDA-approved reference product for a specific indication — tesamorelin for HIV-associated lipodystrophy, Melanotan I (afamelanotide) for erythropoietic protoporphyria, PT-141 (bremelanotide) for hypoactive sexual desire disorder in premenopausal women — but the approval attaches to that product, not to the compounded preparation you order here, and not to any other indication.
What is the actual difference between 503A and 503B? +
A 503A compounding pharmacy prepares a drug for an identified individual patient against a prescription. A 503B outsourcing facility may compound without patient-specific prescriptions, produces under current good manufacturing practice (CGMP), and registers with FDA. Registration is not approval. Neither pathway produces an FDA-approved drug product, and neither is reviewed by FDA for safety or efficacy before marketing. What the distinction changes for you is operational: who the order is written for, whether the practice can hold stock, and what your state board permits.
Can product ship to my clinic, or does it have to go to the patient? +
Both channels can ship either way. The 503A channel ships to the clinic or to the patient, with one important exception: certain products, testosterone among them, must ship direct to the patient and never to the clinic. The 503A/B channel ships direct to the patient or to the clinic. Your state pharmacy and medical-board rules govern what the practice may hold and administer, and they differ by state — confirm yours before planning around office stock.
Is any of this research-use-only material? +
No. Nothing in this catalog is supplied as research-use-only material. Every item comes through a licensed 503A or 503A/B channel and is dispensed against a prescription. That is the whole reason the program is structured the way it is. Is compounded semaglutide the same as the branded product? +
