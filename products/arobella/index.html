FDA 510(k)-Cleared · Ultrasonic Debridement & Cleansing

Qoustic Wound Therapy System™ — a curette driven at 35 kHz.

The AR1000 Series from Arobella Medical, LLC puts a dome-shaped, sharp-edged titanium curette on a 35 kHz low-frequency ultrasound generator with integrated irrigation — one FDA 510(k)-cleared platform (K131096) for contact debridement, non-contact maintenance debridement, wound cleansing, and graft-bed preparation. The cutting edge of the Qoustic Qurette is literal: clinicians work with an instrument they already know, while a calibrated cavitation field helps separate necrotic tissue from healthy tissue. Albacete MedDev distributes the system to US practices with coding and documentation support.

How it works

Familiar in the hand, different at the edge.

Arobella builds the system from three core parts — signal generator, hand piece, and disposable tubing — and the hand piece starts with a curette, so its use feels familiar. Per Arobella, the QWTS is the only ultrasound wound-therapy device operating at 35 kHz, the frequency it calls the "Sweet Spot" for bioburden control.

Generator

AR1000B signal generator

Runs on standard wall power (90–264 VAC, 50–60 Hz) and sends the ultrasonic signal to the hand piece over a conductive cable. Converter output stays under 140 watts, with amplitude adjustable from 0 to 75 µm across a 0–100% power range.

Hand piece

The Qoustic Qurette

A converter with a Ti-6Al-4V transducer horn and Ti-6Al-4V Qurette probe tips. The curette's unique dome shape emits and focuses the ultrasound, and the hand piece is calibrated to create a stable cavitation field that helps separate necrotic tissue from healthy tissue. For denser tissue, the cutting edge of the curette does the cutting — and the head keeps performing under significant load.

Fluid wash

Irrigation that carries ultrasound

Disposable tubing delivers an integrated fluid wash to the Qurette tip, cleansing the work area. Because sound travels better through liquid than air, the wash also increases delivery of ultrasonic energy to the wound — a capability Arobella patented (US Patent No. 8,050,752, 2011, to co-founder Dr. Eliaz Babaev).

Indications for Use — as cleared

The AR1000 Series and its variants produce and deliver low frequency ultrasound used to promote wound healing via:

  • Selective and non-selective dissection and fragmentation of soft and/or hard tissue
  • Surgical, excisional or sharp-edge wound debridement (acute and chronic wounds, burns) for the removal of nonviable tissue including but not limited to diseased tissue, necrotic tissue, slough and eschar, fibrin, tissue exudates, bacteria and other matter
  • Site cleansing irrigation and lavage of wound tissue (acute and chronic wounds, burns, diseased or necrotic tissue)
  • Contact and/or non-contact maintenance debridement for the removal of debris, exudates, fragments, bacteria, slough, fibrin, excised or fragmented tissue, and other matter
  • Preparing the wound bed for graft or other subsequent procedures using contact and/or non-contact techniques to achieve wound debridement

Patient population is patients of any age with one or more wounds, and may also exhibit diabetes mellitus (DM). Irrigation fluid may be sterile de-ionized water, sterile saline solution, or another approved wound therapy or debridement solution. Arobella states it is the only company with the graft-bed-preparation indication for use, and positions the system for diabetic foot ulcers, pressure ulcers, venous insufficiency ulcers, arterial wounds, compromised surgical wounds, burns, osteomyelitis, fistulas, and infected, eczematous, ulcerated or devitalized skin.

Evidence

Strong at the bench, small series in clinic.

Arobella's bibliography is deepest in vitro; clinical support is a retrospective chart review, a two-patient case report, and manufacturer case studies. Arobella reports use at Mayo Clinic, Walter Reed Military Medical Center, Johns Hopkins Bayview Medical Center, Duke Medicine, and Baylor University Medical Center, and a first-place finish in Podiatry Today's Top Ten Innovations of 2010.

In vitro

Bioburden and cell behavior

University of Winston-Salem researchers used Arobella equipment to eliminate MRSA in vitro at 35 kHz (Conner-Kerr et al., 2010, Ostomy Wound Management). At Mayo Clinic College of Medicine, Karau et al. (2010) ran the QWTS against Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Staphylococcus epidermidis, and Staphylococcus aureus — planktonic bacteria eliminated, biofilm bacteria reduced. Conner-Kerr’s group later reported altered human-fibroblast migration with faster scratch-model closure (2015). Bench findings — not patient outcomes.

Clinical

A chart review, two cases, case studies

A retrospective chart review at The Regional Medical Center in South Carolina reported reduced chronic-wound pain in patients with diabetic ulcers, on equipment originally developed by Dr. Babaev (Gehling & Samies, 2007). A two-patient venous-insufficiency report — a 63-year-old with two wounds over 12 months old, a 77-year-old with seven wounds — documented improvement ranging to full healing (Maher et al., 2014). Arobella's case studies add limb salvage at two treatments per week, an amputation site ready for prosthetic fitting nine months ahead of schedule after three months of treatment (East Alabama Medical Center, n=1), graft-bed preparation before Apligraf, and biofilm removal.

The honest read

Mechanism-rich, RCT-light

Manufacturer-reported comparisons describe a statistically significant blood-flow increase versus traditional sharp debridement and faster healing in treated groups — designs and sample sizes are not stated. Two aerosolization studies reported less splatter and aerosolization, and no contamination in the surrounding area. No comparative randomized trial of the QWTS itself appears in the materials we distribute; Arobella's bibliography also spans low-frequency-ultrasound studies of other systems, including noncontact MIST therapy. Weigh the platform on its cleared indications, bench bioburden data, and small clinical series.

Reading the families

97597 describes debridement of an open wound with sharp selective instruments — scissors, scalpel, forceps — per session, first 20 sq cm or less; add-on 97598 covers each additional 20 sq cm. 97610 describes low frequency, non-contact, non-thermal ultrasound, per day — the descriptor that matches the QWTS non-contact mode. Surgical debridement steps by depth: subcutaneous tissue (11042, +11045), muscle and/or fascia (11043, +11046), bone (11044, +11047). Burn codes 16020–16030 scale small to large by body-surface area; 15002–15004 cover surgical preparation of a graft recipient site.

FAQ

Common questions, direct answers.

What physicians and practice administrators ask us about the Qoustic Wound Therapy System™.

Is the Qoustic system FDA approved? +
No — "approved" is the wrong word for this pathway. The AR1000 Series is FDA 510(k)-cleared: K131096, dated May 16, 2014, which expanded the indications first cleared under K062544 (January 3, 2007; revised July 2, 2009). FDA classifies it as Class II under 21 CFR 878.4410, product code NRB. It is a prescription device — US federal law restricts it to sale, distribution, or use by or on the order of a physician, and it is for trained personnel only.
What separates contact from non-contact use? +
Contact means the Qurette itself does the work: sharp-edge and ultrasonic debridement, with the cutting edge of the curette used to cut into denser tissue while the head performs under load. Non-contact means hovering the Qurette 1–2 cm above the tissue, letting the fluid wash couple the ultrasound to the wound for maintenance debridement and cleansing. Arobella's technique guidance calls for light contact or hover, working in a mesh or spiral-out pattern.
Which irrigation fluids can it run? +
Sterile 0.9% saline — widely available from medical suppliers and supplied by the user — plus sterile de-ionized water, Vashe™ Wound Cleanser solution, or other solutions approved for wound therapy or debridement.
How are QWTS procedures coded? +
Arobella's 2024 quick reference lists selective debridement (97597, +97598) and non-selective debridement (97602) per session, low-frequency non-contact non-thermal ultrasound (97610) per day, surgical debridement by depth (11042–11047), partial-thickness burn care (16020–16030), and recipient-site preparation (15002–15004). Code selection rests with the billing clinician, and nothing here is a promise of coverage or payment. Albacete MedDev provides coding and documentation support for each account.
What does it take to get a program running? +
Two requirements, per Arobella: the clinician must be authorized by the facility to do wound management, and staff earn Arobella's QWTS Certification — a few hours of hands-on training covering equipment use, maintenance, and sterile handling, delivered onsite as part of the purchase. The cleared regimen is a single treatment, no more than three times per week or as the physician directs, typically about two weeks or more.
