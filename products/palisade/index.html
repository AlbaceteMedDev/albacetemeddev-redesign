361 HCT/P · Dual-Membrane Placental Allograft

Palisade® — both placental membranes, one allograft.

Palisade® Placental-Derived Allograft from Sequence LifeScience, Inc. is a dual-membrane allograft comprised of the amniotic and chorionic membranes of the placenta — an extracellular-matrix protective barrier or wound covering for acute and chronic cutaneous wounds. Minimally manipulated, dehydrated, and terminally sterilized to a 10⁻⁶ sterility assurance level. Albacete MedDev supplies Palisade to US practices with coding and documentation support.

Dual-membrane construction

Two membranes, one graft.

Placental tissue is newly formed extracellular matrix, built during pregnancy to protect and support fetal development — a native ECM structure. It carries two distinct layers, and Palisade™ retains both. Sequence LifeScience positions the graft for a variety of acute or chronic wound applications, particularly when applied to moderate-to-deep wound beds.

Amnion

The fetus-facing layer

The internal membrane of the placenta. A thinner yet more compact layer of extracellular matrix, positioned against the developing fetus.

Chorion

The maternal-facing layer

The external membrane. Slightly thicker but less compact than the amnion — the two layers differ subtly because of the positions they occupy in the placenta.

Shared chemistry

One protein family, both layers

Both membranes are constituted of collagen fibers — predominantly types I, III, IV and V — along with laminin, fibronectin, proteoglycans and hyaluronic acid.

What the processing preserves

Palisade™ allograft tissues are minimally manipulated and dehydrated, and retain relevant structural extracellular-matrix characteristics of the starting placental membranes. Donor eligibility determination, screening, and testing are performed in accordance with applicable FDA requirements and the standards of AATB, the Association for Advancing Tissue and Biologics.

  • Donated tissue comes from fully consented mothers undergoing full-term C-sections.
  • All tissue is obtained in partnership with FDA-regulated and accredited recovery organizations.
  • Finished grafts are terminally sterilized by validated methods to a sterility assurance level of 10⁻⁶.
  • Screening-procedure details are published in the package insert and Instructions for Use.
Evidence

What the citations show — and what they don't.

Reference 2

Roy et al., 2022

"Placental Tissues as Biomaterials in Regenerative Medicine," BioMed Research International, vol. 2022, pp. 1–26. A 26-page article on placental tissues as a biomaterial class.

The honest read

Mechanism rationale, not trial data

These references characterize placental membranes generally; neither is a clinical trial of Palisade™, and no Palisade-specific clinical outcome data appears in the manufacturer materials we distribute. Weigh the graft on its documented construction, processing, and 361 HCT/P status.

FAQ

Common questions, direct answers.

What physicians and practice administrators ask us about Palisade™.

Is Palisade FDA approved? +
No — and that is true of every product in this class. Palisade™ Placental-Derived Allograft is regulated as a human cell, tissue, and cellular and tissue-based product (HCT/P) under Section 361 and FDA 21 CFR Part 1271. That pathway involves no premarket approval or clearance, so the terms "FDA approved" and "FDA cleared" do not apply to it. What the pathway does require: donor eligibility determination, screening, and testing in accordance with applicable FDA requirements.
What is Palisade intended for? +
Palisade™ Placental-Derived Allograft is intended as a natural biologic wound covering for cutaneous wounds — acute or chronic. It is for use by qualified health care professionals in a physician office, outpatient, or inpatient setting.
How do the two membranes differ? +
Position drives the difference. The amnion is the internal, fetus-facing membrane — a thinner yet more compact layer of extracellular matrix. The chorion is the external, maternal-facing membrane — slightly thicker but less compact. Both layers are built from the same protein families: collagen types I, III, IV and V, with laminin, fibronectin, proteoglycans and hyaluronic acid. Palisade™ is comprised of both membranes, and the finished tissue retains relevant structural ECM characteristics of the starting placental membranes.
How is Palisade billed? +
Under HCPCS Level II code Q4350 — "Palisade™ Matrix, per square centimeter." Four sizes from 4 cm² to 32 cm² let you match graft area to the wound. Albacete MedDev supports coding and documentation for each order; we do not promise coverage or payment amounts, which rest with each payer.
Is there Palisade-specific clinical outcome data? +
Not in the materials we distribute. The manufacturer's brochure cites two publications on placental tissue science (Niknejad 2008; Roy 2022) that support the structure and composition claims — neither is a Palisade™ clinical trial. If your program requires product-specific evidence, tell us and we will request current documentation from Sequence LifeScience.
