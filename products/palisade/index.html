Placental Tissue

Tissue that was built to protect — a neomatrix.

Placental tissue was previously considered biologic waste following delivery. It is a pristine, newly formed extracellular matrix whose whole job in utero was to protect and support fetal development — which is why the manufacturer describes it as an untainted extracellular matrix, essentially a neomatrix.

Origin

Newly formed ECM

Placental tissue was previously considered biologic waste following delivery. The manufacturer’s literature describes it instead as a pristine, newly formed extracellular matrix — recovered from fully consented mothers at full-term Caesarean delivery.

Native function

A protective envelope

In utero the placental membranes exist to protect and support fetal development. The barrier and wound-cover role Palisade™ is designed for draws on the structural and biochemical properties of that tissue.

The consequence

An untainted matrix

Because it is a newly formed matrix built for fetal development, placental tissue is described in the manufacturer’s literature as an untainted extracellular matrix — essentially a neomatrix. That is the structural argument behind the material.

Background references cited in the manufacturer’s literature: Niknejad H, Peirovi H, Jorjani M, et al. “Properties of the Amniotic Membrane for Potential Use in Tissue Engineering,” Eur Cell Mater, vol. 15, pp. 88–99, 2008. Roy A, Mantay M, Brannan C, Griffiths S. “Placental Tissues as Biomaterials in Regenerative Medicine,” BioMed Research International, vol. 2022, pp. 1–26, 2022.

Descriptions of Layers

One placenta. Two membranes.

Broadly, placental tissue includes an internal, fetus-facing amnion and an external, maternal-facing chorion. Their physical and biochemical properties are subtly different — owing to the position each occupies in the placenta. Palisade™ keeps both.

What the second membrane adds

The two membranes are not duplicates of one another. The amnion is the thinner, more compact matrix; the chorion is slightly thicker and less compact. Carrying both is what separates Palisade™ from the single-layer amnion graft we carry alongside it — and the manufacturer positions Palisade™ particularly for moderate-to-deep wound beds.

  • Position drives propertyThe physical and biochemical differences between the two membranes follow from where each sits in the placenta — internal and fetus-facing, or external and maternal-facing.
  • Compact and openA more compact amniotic matrix and a less compact chorionic matrix in one graft, rather than one architecture on its own.
  • Shared biochemistryBoth layers are constituted of collagen fibers — predominantly Types I, III, IV and V — along with laminin, fibronectin, proteoglycans and hyaluronic acid.
  • Characteristics retainedPalisade™ allograft tissues retain the structural and functional characteristics of the starting placental membranes.
  • Minimally manipulatedThe finished products are minimally manipulated and dehydrated, derived from human amniotic and chorionic membranes of the placenta.
  • Terminally sterilizedFinal products are packaged in different sizes and verified to be terminally sterilized to a defined sterility assurance level (SAL).

Read the construction, not the marketing. Dual-membrane means amnion and chorion in the same graft. The manufacturer’s brochure describes the layers and their composition; it does not assign a separate clinical function to each constituent, and neither do we.

Matrix Composition

Four collagen types. Four named companions.

The manufacturer’s literature gives one composition for both primary membranes: collagen fibers, predominantly Types I, III, IV and V, along with other ECM proteins including laminin, fibronectin, proteoglycans and hyaluronic acid. That is a description of the material — not a claim about what any single constituent does in your patient’s wound.

Structural

Collagen I, III, IV, V

Both layers are constituted of collagen fibers, predominantly Types I, III, IV and V. The same four types are described for the amnion and for the chorion; what differs between them is compaction and thickness, not the collagen list.

Adhesive glycoproteins

Laminin & fibronectin

Named in the manufacturer’s literature among the other ECM proteins present alongside the collagen fibers in both membranes.

Also present

Proteoglycans & HA

Proteoglycans and hyaluronic acid complete the stated composition — again, for both the amniotic and the chorionic membrane.

The manufacturer’s brochure lists these constituents; it does not attribute a specific clinical effect to any one of them, and it presents no clinical outcome data. Properties of amniotic membrane and of placental tissues as biomaterials are described in the two references the brochure cites (Niknejad 2008; Roy 2022) — neither is a trial of this product.

How the decision actually gets made

The depth language in both brochures is manufacturer positioning — not a coverage rule and not an indication limit. Both products carry the same stated intended use: a natural biologic wound covering or skin substitute for cutaneous wounds. Graft selection remains the treating clinician’s judgment.

  • DepthPalisade™ is positioned for moderate-to-deep beds; Sentry™ SL for shallow-to-moderate. That is the line the product literature draws between them.
  • ConstructionDual-membrane amnion + chorion versus a single amniotic layer — a material difference, not a marketing tier.
  • CodingTwo distinct HCPCS Level II codes, Q4350 and Q4348. They are not interchangeable on a claim.
  • SizingIdentical four-size ladder across both, so a practice can switch depth strategy without relearning the size chart.
  • ManufacturerPalisade™ is made by Nvision Biomedical Technologies; Sentry™ SL by Sequence LifeScience. Two companies, one distribution channel.

Safety & Sourcing

Consented donors, screened donors, then terminal sterilization.

Four controls stand between the delivery room and the sterile package. None of them is optional, and all four are stated in the manufacturer’s literature.

01 · Consent

Fully consented donors

Donated tissues for Palisade™ products are collected from fully consented mothers undergoing full-term c-sections. Consent and a full-term Caesarean delivery are both collection criteria — not preferences.

02 · Screening

FDA and AATB standards

Each donor is screened according to the strict standards required by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration and the American Association of Tissue Banks. Additional detail on screening procedures is in the package insert and Instructions for Use.

03 · Recovery

Accredited recovery partners

All donated tissue is obtained in partnership with FDA-regulated and accredited recovery organizations — the chain of custody starts inside a regulated system, not outside one.

04 · Sterilization

Validated terminal process

Palisade™ products undergo a validated terminal sterilization process, and final products are verified to a defined sterility assurance level. Terminal — meaning in the final package, not before it.

Regulatory status — read this before the first case

Palisade™ Placental-Derived Allograft products are 361 HCT/Ps — human cells, tissues, and cellular and tissue-based products regulated as human tissue under 21 CFR Part 1271. They are not FDA-approved and they are not 510(k)-cleared devices; there is no premarket approval or clearance number to cite, because that pathway does not apply to this tier. The products are minimally manipulated and dehydrated, and are intended as a natural biologic wound covering or skin substitute for cutaneous wounds. Any representation to a patient, a payer, or a hospital committee should say exactly that.

What a clean Q4350 claim needs

A per-square-centimeter code is only as defensible as the measurements behind it. The items below are documentation discipline, not payer policy — your payer’s own rules always govern.

  • Units match the graftBilled square centimeters should reconcile to the SKU applied — 4, 8, 16 or 32 cm² for the four Palisade™ sizes.
  • Serial wound measurementsLength, width and depth at each visit, with the trend visible across the record rather than reconstructed after the fact.
  • Conservative care documentedWhat was tried before the graft, for how long, and why it was insufficient.
  • Product identifiers in the noteSKU, size and package identifiers recorded with the application.
  • Site of service correctOffice, HOPD and inpatient application are billed on different pathways — confirm before the first application, not after the first denial.
  • Discarded portion handled per policyIf any portion is not applied, record it and follow your payer’s instructions for reporting it.

We do the coding walkthrough before the first case. Our revenue-cycle team confirms current Q4350 status with your MAC or commercial payer, reviews your note template against the documentation above, and stays on the file if a claim is reviewed.

How to read it

Material, not outcome

These references support statements about what placental tissue is — its composition, its structure, its properties as a biomaterial. They do not establish a healing rate, a time-to-closure, or a comparison against any other graft.

No comparative-efficacy claim is made on this page against any other skin substitute or placental allograft, because the manufacturer’s literature makes none. If a competing product is on your formulary and you want a like-for-like technical comparison, ask us and we will build one from published labeling — not from marketing copy.
