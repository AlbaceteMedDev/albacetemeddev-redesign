Where it sits

Two portfolios. One standard.

Read this page as a physician and the first question is the right one: is this the graft line under a different name? It is not. Ovena Health is a separate, consumer-facing brand with its own catalog, its own prices, and its own channels. The clinical portfolio does not move to retail, and Ovena does not move into the OR.

Provider-only

The clinical portfolio

Placental and ECM allografts, engineered and xenograft matrices, surgical and autologous biologics, ultrasound and NPWT devices, and compounded peptide therapeutics. Ordered by a practice or facility, applied by a clinician, documented and coded through the payer. Nothing in that portfolio is sold to a patient at retail.

Ovena Health

Seven products, published prices, shipped to a home address. Collagen dressings, powder and kit, a cut-to-size hydrocolloid roll, 20–30 mmHg compression, a donning aid, and sterile saline wound wash — bought on a card, not billed through a chart.

Ovena Health is Albacete MedDev’s own brand rather than a manufacturer line we distribute — which is why this page carries retail prices where our product pages carry codes. Nothing here is a substitute for clinical judgement, and nothing here should be read as a claim about the provider-only portfolio.

What “510(k) cleared” means here

The collagen dressing is cleared — not approved. Those are different regulatory pathways and the distinction matters to anyone reading a chart. The language used on the live Amazon listing is precise about it, and we hold to the same wording.

  • Cleared as a device“cleared by FDA as a medical device in its own right, not merely produced in a registered facility”
  • The MMP rationaleChronic wounds carry elevated matrix metalloproteinases; Type I bovine collagen dressings bind and reduce MMPs — a mechanism the listing describes as documented in 300+ peer-reviewed studies and reflected in WOCN, AAWC and EWMA chronic wound guidance
  • HCPCS A6021“collagen dressing, sterile, 16 sq in or less” — noted on the Amazon listing for patient records
  • What it does not replace“Collagen supports wound healing; offloading, compression, and pressure redistribution still apply”

On the code. A6021 is listed so a patient has it for their own records — a retail purchase is not a claim. Where a code does get billed, coverage and payment are determined by CMS or the payer and are subject to change.

Common questions

What to know about Ovena Health.

Is Ovena Health the same thing as your clinical graft portfolio? +
No, and the distinction is deliberate. Ovena Health is Albacete MedDev’s consumer and retail brand — seven products with published prices, bought by a patient or a practice and shipped to an address. The clinical graft portfolio is provider-only: ordered by a practice or facility, applied by a clinician, and documented through the chart. The two do not overlap. If you are evaluating tissue products for your practice, you want the product pages, not this one.
Is the collagen dressing FDA approved? +
It is FDA 510(k) cleared, which is not the same as approved, and we do not use the word “approved” about it. The distinction the live listing draws is worth repeating: it is “cleared by FDA as a medical device in its own right, not merely produced in a registered facility.” The dressing is 100% Type I bovine collagen and is described for the management of pressure ulcers, venous ulcers, diabetic foot ulcers, surgical wounds, and other partial- and full-thickness wounds.
Do the compression socks prevent blood clots? +
We do not make that claim. The socks are described as 20–30 mmHg graduated compression for daily edema management and venous support. Where clot risk comes up in a compression conversation, the honest framing is risk reduction, never prevention — and whether compression is appropriate for a given patient, at what strength, is a decision for the treating clinician. Ovena products do not treat or cure disease.
Can I pay with an FSA or HSA? +
Ovena products are FSA/HSA eligible, but eligibility is decided by your plan, not by us — verify with your plan administrator before you count on it. For the collagen dressing, HCPCS A6021 (“collagen dressing, sterile, 16 sq in or less”) is noted on the Amazon listing so you have it for your own records. A retail purchase is not an insurance claim; where a code is billed, coverage and payment are set by CMS or the payer and are subject to change.
Should I buy on ovenahealth.com or on Amazon? +
Buy direct when you want the full range in one order — every dressing and sock variant, the Collagen Kit, the Sock Aid, the Sterile Saline Wound Wash, and the Juzo partner stockings — or when your order clears the $75 free-shipping threshold. Buy on Amazon when you would rather use an account you already have: as of August 17, 2026 the storefront pages we confirmed were the collagen dressings, the hydrocolloid roll in both lengths and the 20–30 mmHg socks — the storefront lists further items, so check it for anything else you need. Pricing tracks between the two — the 4×4 collagen dressing is $55.00 either way — and ovenahealth.com is the canonical source if they ever differ.
Who reviews these products before they go on sale? +
Dr. David Chahine, MD, Ovena’s Medical Director. He is a practicing wound-care physician trained at Stanford University School of Medicine, and every product in the line is clinically reviewed by him. That is the whole premise of the brand: “Clinical rigor, honest access, and credentialed care, in every product line.”
Can a practice buy Ovena for its patients? +
Yes — the brand is priced for both, which is what “whether patient or practice” means. A practice buys the same catalog at the same published prices, with free shipping over $75, and does not need a distribution account to keep a consistent dressing or compression item on the shelf.
