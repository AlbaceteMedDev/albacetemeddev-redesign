02Materials & membranes

Tissue source, structure and processing.

A membrane, a particulate matrix and an autologous clot differ in source, structure and preparation. Those differences are the starting point for a product review.

For a placental membrane, identify the tissues retained, the processing method and the supplied format. Then look for clinical research on that exact product.

05Notes on the evidence

Selected clinical studies.

These two papers illustrate why the study design, denominator and endpoint belong next to a reported result. They are selected reading from the broader evidence library.

Microlyte / Prospective pilot / 2020

A bioresorbable matrix in complex chronic wounds

Manning and colleagues evaluated 32 wounds after losses to follow-up. At 12 weeks, 29 had either healed completely or improved significantly. Established care, including debridement or compression when prescribed, continued during the study.

Interpretation: This was an open-label study without a concurrent control group. The combined outcome cannot be read as a complete-closure rate.

What to examine in the paper

Separate complete closure from improvement, note the mixed wound types, and review the care continued alongside the matrix.

Manning SW et al. Wounds. 2020;32(11):309–318.

Read the paper on PubMed ↗

“Healed or improved” and “healed completely” answer different questions.

The distinction matters when discussing the 12-week result in the Manning pilot study.

APIS / Retrospective comparison / 2023

A collagen-based sheet in a hospital wound clinic

Rodríguez and colleagues compared 47 chronic wounds: 27 treated with the bioengineered sheet and 20 with standard care. Among wounds that closed, mean time to closure was 7.4 and 14.8 weeks, respectively.

Interpretation: Most wounds in both groups did not close. The retrospective design and the analysis of wounds that closed limit how broadly the time-to-closure finding can be applied.

What to examine in the paper

Review the treatments included in standard care, visit adherence and the reasons some wounds remained open.

Rodríguez I et al. Wounds. 2023;35(1):E35–E38.
