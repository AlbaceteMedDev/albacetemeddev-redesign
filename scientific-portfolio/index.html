The Chronic Wound Problem

Why chronic wounds fail to heal.

Chronic wounds are locked in a self-amplifying proteolytic cycle. MMPs at 30× physiological levels destroy growth factors, native ECM, and their own inhibitors. Biofilm maintains inflammation. Senescent fibroblasts amplify the cascade.

MMP Overexpression

MMP-9 in poor healers: 17× higher. PDGF degraded within 2 hours.

Biofilm Persistence

60–90% of chronic wounds. EPS matrix shields bacteria from immunity.

Growth Factor Sink

Proteolytic environment degrades growth factors before signaling can occur.

Cellular Senescence

>15% senescent cells = treatment-resistant. SASP amplifies MMPs.

Product Portfolio — By Complexity

HC-HA/PTX3 anti-inflammatory master switch

HA >4,000 kDa + heavy chain 1 via TSG-6 + pentraxin 3 drives apoptosis of activated neutrophils/macrophages, M2 polarization, and Treg expansion. Downregulates TGF-β1 mRNA + receptors I/II/III and activates BMP — reverting myofibroblasts and attenuating nociceptive excitability.

Polymer science

PEM nanofilm architecture

Layer-by-layer cationic/anionic polymer assembly. Cationic termination enhances cell attachment, ECM production, and actin organization via electrostatic interaction with proteoglycans.

PVA cytophilicity

Hydroxyl (–OH) groups hydrogen-bond with ECM proteins (fibronectin, vitronectin). Presents integrin-binding domains. Mechanical properties mimic native soft tissue.

Bioresorbable design

~20 µm PVA resorbs in 7 days. No removal → no granulation disruption. Integrates without foreign-body response. 4-year shelf life.

Collagen Science

Type I Collagen — sacrificial substrate & bioactive signal.

Exogenous collagen reverses the proteolytic cycle by diverting MMP activity from endogenous ECM. As it degrades, it releases bioactive matrikines that actively drive granulation and fibroblast chemotaxis.

ECM & Matrix Science

Acellular matrices: four routes to the same goal.

When the proteolytic environment has been reset, the wound needs scaffold — structure a cell can attach to, migrate across, and remodel. The portfolio carries four distinct matrix chemistries, each matched to a wound geometry rather than competing for the same one.

Placental sheets

Amnion & chorion membranes

Collagen types I, III, IV and V with laminin, fibronectin, proteoglycans and hyaluronic acid — the native neomatrix, minimally manipulated and terminally sterilized. Palisade® carries both membranes; Sentry™ SL is the thin, conforming single layer. 361 HCT/Ps.

Palisade & Sentry →
Particulate CTM

Chorionic-plate connective tissue

Interfyl® decellularizes the chorionic plate into flowable and particulate formats that conform to irregular defects and tunnels — matrix where a sheet cannot reach. Billed per mg under Q4171.

Interfyl →
Multi-tissue ECM

Lung + spleen particulate

XCelliStem™ blends two organ ECMs for a broader protein mix than single-tissue powders — manufacturer-reported collagen/elastin/fibronectin/laminin composition. FDA 510(k)-cleared (K172593), billed per mg under A2004.

Preparation & Adjunctive Care

The cascade resets on a prepared bed.

Matrix placed on slough, biofilm and stalled tissue is matrix wasted. The conservative-care side of the portfolio handles what comes before and after the graft: debridement, cleansing, and pressure management.

Contact ultrasonic

Qoustic Wound Therapy System™

A curette driven at 35 kHz debrides, excises and cleanses in one instrument, with irrigation through the handpiece. FDA 510(k)-cleared (K131096); bench studies report planktonic elimination and biofilm reduction — bench findings, not patient outcomes.

Arobella Qoustic →
Non-contact NLFU

UltraMist® Therapy

Low-frequency ultrasound delivered through a saline mist — painless, non-contact wound cleansing and maintenance debridement (K050129, K140782), used adjunctively alongside standard care under CPT 97610.

UltraMist →
Negative pressure

MicroDoc Disposable NPWT

Single-use negative pressure sized for outpatient and home health — exudate management and tissue approximation without rental logistics. NPWT’s evidence base is modality-level; we say so plainly on the product page.

A typical sequence

Debride and cleanse (Qoustic or UltraMist) → neutralize the proteolytic environment (collagen systems, APIS®) → control bioburden (Microlyte SAM) → graft the matrix the geometry calls for (Matrix Science tab) → manage exudate and approximation (MicroDoc NPWT). Sequence and selection remain clinical judgments — this is the portfolio’s map, not a protocol.
